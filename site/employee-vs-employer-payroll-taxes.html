Employee vs employer payroll taxes

Two sets of lines on one paycheck: one comes out of pay, one is a cost.

A paycheck carries two kinds of tax. Employee taxes are withheld from the employee's pay: federal income tax, state and local income tax, the employee's share of Social Security and Medicare, and state employee contributions. Employer taxes are paid by the employer on top: the matching Social Security and Medicare, federal unemployment tax, state unemployment tax, and in some states paid-leave contributions. In the API each line says which: payer.

Why the split matters

  • Net pay depends only on employee taxes.
  • Employer cost depends on both. See the true cost of an employee.
  • A small number of states charge employees for programs where most charge employers, so check the state page.

Questions

Which payroll taxes does the employer pay?

The employer's match of Social Security and Medicare, federal unemployment tax, state unemployment tax in most states, and some state paid-leave contributions. They are paid on top of gross pay.

Does the API show which side pays?

Yes. Each tax line has a payer field, employee or employer, and the response totals them separately.
