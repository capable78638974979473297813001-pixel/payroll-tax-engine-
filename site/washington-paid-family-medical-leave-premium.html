Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave premium

A shared premium on wages up to a cap, with no state income tax beside it.

Washington has no personal income tax, but it funds Paid Family and Medical Leave through a premium on wages. The premium is a percentage of wages up to the Social Security wage base, split between the employer and employee in shares that depend on the employer's size.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in Washington

The engine returns $24.21 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 0.81%, capped at $184500.00/yr ($0.00 YTD already counted)

Near the yearly limit the contribution stops. With $183,500.00 already counted this year against a $184,500.00 limit, only $1,000.00 of the same $3,000.00 check is left to charge, and the line becomes $8.07 (against $24.21 early in the year). The engine explains it: $1000.00 @ 0.81%, capped at $184500.00/yr ($183500.00 YTD already counted)

Who pays

Shared between employee and employer. The employee line is the employee's share of the total premium; larger employers pay a bigger portion.

Points to remember

  • The premium rate and the wage cap are set each year.
  • Washington has no income tax line, so PFML and the Washington Cares premium are the only state-specific employee lines.
  • Track year-to-date in ytd.statePaidLeave.WA.
  • Employer size changes the split, which is an employer fact.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to WA, as WA_PFML_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See Washington payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

Does Washington have a state income tax?

No. Washington does not tax wages. Its employee payroll deductions are PFML and the Washington Cares Fund premium.

Who pays the PFML premium?

Both. The total premium is split between employer and employee, with the shares depending on employer size.
