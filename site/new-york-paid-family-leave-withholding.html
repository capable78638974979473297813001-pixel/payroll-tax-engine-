New York Paid Family Leave withholding

An employee contribution with a yearly dollar cap.

New York Paid Family Leave (PFL) is funded by an employee contribution withheld from wages, capped at a yearly maximum set by the state. Employers insure the benefit but may recover the contribution only from employees, up to the cap.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in New York

The engine returns $12.96 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 0.43%, capped at $95348.76/yr ($0.00 YTD already counted)

Near the yearly limit the contribution stops. With $94,348.76 already counted this year against a $95,348.76 limit, only $1,000.00 of the same $3,000.00 check is left to charge, and the line becomes $4.32 (against $12.96 early in the year). The engine explains it: $1000.00 @ 0.43%, capped at $95348.76/yr ($94348.76 YTD already counted)

Who pays

The employee, by payroll deduction, up to the annual maximum. The employer arranges the coverage.

Points to remember

  • The cap is a yearly dollar amount, so high earners stop paying part-way through the year.
  • It is separate from the Disability Benefits Law (DBL) deduction, which has its own tiny weekly cap.
  • Track year-to-date in ytd.statePaidLeave.NY.
  • Employers in the New York City commuter zone also have quarterly MCTMT; see the employer-taxes endpoint.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to NY, as NY_PFML_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See New York payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

How much is New York PFL?

It is a percentage of wages up to a yearly cap set by the state. The engine states the rate and cap on each line and applies the cap using year-to-date wages.

Does the employer pay PFL?

The employer arranges coverage, but the contribution is withheld from the employee, up to the yearly maximum.
