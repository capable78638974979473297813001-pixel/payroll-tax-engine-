Withholding and wages

Final paycheck and PTO payout withholding

The last check is still a paycheck, with a payout riding on it.

A final paycheck has no special tax rules. The last period's wages and any payout of unused paid time off are wages, subject to the same withholding as any other check. The payout is usually treated as supplemental wages. What changes is that the check often hits a cap, because it is a final year-to-date total.

An example

An employee's last check is $3,000.00 of regular pay plus a $4,000.00 payout of unused PTO.

Method for the payoutFederal withheldNet pay
Flat supplemental rate$1,200.38$5,264.12
Aggregate with the regular pay$1,246.69$5,217.81

Social Security and Medicare are $434.00 and $101.50 either way.

Timing is a state matter

When the final pay is due, and whether unused PTO must be paid, are set by state law and employer policy. The tax engine calculates the withholding on whatever amount you decide is owed. It does not decide the amount or the deadline.

Watch for

  • Year-to-date wages. A final check can reach the Social Security wage base or the Additional Medicare threshold.
  • Employer unemployment tax. Wages paid reduce the remaining state wage base.
  • Garnishments. A final check can still be subject to a withholding order; see the garnishment limits.
  • Reimbursements of business expenses. Paid under an accountable plan they are not wages; send them as reimbursement.

Questions

Is PTO payout taxed differently?

It is wages, usually treated as supplemental: the flat 22% federal rate if identified separately, or aggregated. Social Security and Medicare apply in full.

Does the API decide when the final pay is due?

No. The due date and whether PTO must be paid are set by state law and employer policy. The API calculates tax on the amount you send.
