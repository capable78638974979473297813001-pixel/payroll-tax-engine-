Payroll tax data quality notices

Honest about which numbers are firm and which need a second look.

Most tax rates can be read straight from the government body that levies them. A few cannot: a city that publishes a rate only in a council minute, a county table that conflicts with another source. A tax engine can hide that or say it. Omnia.tax says it, in a notices array on the response, so your product can decide what to do.

What a notice contains

  • taxId: which line the notice is about.
  • tier: how the rate is sourced, for example secondary_source, inferred, not_modelled or conflicting_sources.
  • note: a sentence in plain language.

What to do with it

  • Show a small flag next to the line in a payroll preview, so the person approving the run can see it.
  • Route paychecks with a notice to a review queue if your process needs a second pair of eyes.
  • Log the notice with the calculation so an audit can see what was known at the time.
  • Do not drop the paycheck. A notice is information, not an error.

Why this beats silence

An engine that returns a confident number for every town looks complete and is quietly wrong in the places where nobody has checked. An engine that says "this rate is from a municipal league survey, not the town's ordinance" lets you verify it once and move on. That is the behaviour a payroll team can build a process around.

Related signals

warnings are separate: they flag inputs that need a second look and provisional-year notices. Coverage is published openly on the coverage page, including which states have local tax listed in full and which are partial.

Questions

What is a data quality notice?

A notice on a paycheck response saying a tax line rests on a weaker source than the levying body's own form, with the tax id, how it is sourced and a plain-language note.

Does a notice mean the number is wrong?

No. It means the source is weaker than ideal and worth verifying once. The number is still the engine's best result.
