Exempt from federal withholding on the W-4

Exempt removes one tax line, not all of them.

An employee can write "Exempt" on the Form W-4 if they expect to owe no federal income tax this year, under conditions set out on the form. The employer then withholds no federal income tax. But exempt status applies to income tax only. FICA still applies, and so can state tax.

A paycheck, exempt and not

Federal income taxSocial SecurityMedicareNet pay
Not exempt$320.38$186.00$43.50$2,450.12
Exempt$0.00$186.00$43.50$2,770.50

Reading it

On the $3,000.00 check, the federal income tax line of $320.38 disappears. Social Security ($186.00) and Medicare ($43.50) are unchanged, so net pay rises by $320.38 and not by the full amount of the gross.

Things to know

  • An exempt claim must be renewed each year; it lapses if a new form is not given by the date the rules set.
  • It does not exempt the employee from state income tax. Each state has its own certificate and its own exempt rules.
  • An employee who claims exempt but is not entitled to may owe tax and penalties at filing time.

In the request

"federalW4": { "filingStatus": "single", "multipleJobs": false,
  "dependentCredit": 0, "otherIncome": 0, "deductions": 0,
  "extraWithholding": 0, "exempt": true }

Questions

Does exempt on the W-4 stop Social Security and Medicare?

No. Exempt status covers federal income tax withholding only. Social Security and Medicare are still withheld.

Does it stop state income tax?

Not by itself. States have their own withholding certificates and exemption rules.
