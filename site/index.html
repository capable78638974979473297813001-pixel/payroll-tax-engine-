One POST returns the tax lines on a paycheck.

Omnia.tax is a payroll tax API, built as an affordable alternative to Symmetry Software’s engine. You send the wages, the W-4, and where the work happened. The response is each tax line, in integer cents.

It calculates withholding. It does not file returns, and it does not deposit pay. Symmetry’s tax engine is sold with Canada, US territories, and finished multi-state reciprocity. Those three are not claims this engine makes. Territories and Canada are out. An employee who lives in one state and works in another is in progress.

What that request returned

Ran in this repo’s engine, not a sketch. 300000 is $3,000.00. The $240.00 401(k) is 24000. Single filer, 2020 W-4, nothing extra withheld. Work and home are both Columbus, so the city tax is the resident rate. The Ohio unemployment line uses the 3.1% rate on the request. Leave employer off and the engine uses that state’s published new-employer rate, and writes that on the line.

Net pay is 211932 cents, $2,119.32. Columbus municipal tax on this check is 7500 cents.

The engine withholds the higher of the resident rate and the work location’s nonresident rate. For a resident of PSD 700102 that is 3%. The EIT detail on the resident response is:

$3000.00 @ 3.00% (the resident rate is higher) — resident 3.00% (PSD 700102) vs. work-location nonresident 1.00% (PSD 700102, PITTSBURGH CITY)

Leave residencePSD off and the same wages are taxed at the 1% nonresident rate. Detail:

$3000.00 @ 1.00% (the nonresident rate is higher) — resident 0.00% (PSD 88000/out-of-state) vs. work-location nonresident 1.00% (PSD 700102, PITTSBURGH CITY)

That response also carries a notice, tier not_modelled, because the home PSD was missing. It says to set residencePSD, or to set it to 880000 for a home outside Pennsylvania. The $60.00 gap in net pay is the earned income tax. Local services tax is $52.00 a year, split across 26 pays and rounded down to the cent, so both checks show 200 cents. Employer tax on both is 36216 cents. No employer unemployment rate was sent, so that line is Pennsylvania’s published new-employer rate, and the detail says so.

Local tax, in eight states

  • Ohio. Municipal income tax, school districts, and JEDD/JEDZ zones. The 2026 municipality file has 683 rows, the school-district file 214, the zone file 157.
  • Pennsylvania. Act 32 earned income tax and local services tax, keyed by PSD code. The 2026 register has 2,627 jurisdictions.
  • Michigan. City income tax. 24 cities in the 2026 file.
  • Indiana. County income tax. 92 counties in the 2026 file.
  • Kentucky. Occupational license tax, city and county.
  • Alabama. Municipal occupational tax. 29 municipalities in the 2026 file.
  • New York. New York City resident tax, and Yonkers for a resident or for a nonresident who works in Yonkers.
  • Oregon. Metro and Multnomah County, plus employer transit-district tax for TriMet, Lane Transit, Canby, Sandy, Wilsonville SMART, and South Clackamas.

Still open, and how an address gets in

POST /v1/address turns a work address into the state and the certificate a later paycheck needs: an Ohio city, a Pennsylvania PSD, an Indiana county, and the rest of that list. The lookup uses rooftop geocoding when a published address point matches. The response names the precision and who published the point. Resolve someone once, store the certificate, send it on each check.

The same paycheck call can also carry garnishments and a minimum-wage check. They come back on that result. The fields are in the docs. The sandbox posts to /v1/paycheck with a test key.

  • Multi-state is in progress. Home in one state and work in another is not a finished path. Don’t build a pay run on it yet.
  • Filing is out of scope. Nothing here produces a return, a W-2 file, or a payment to a tax agency.
  • Direct deposit is out of scope. Net pay is a number on the response. The API does not move money.
  • Territories and Canada are out of scope. Fifty states and the District of Columbia are the state list.
