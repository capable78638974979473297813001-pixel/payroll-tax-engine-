Hawaii Temporary Disability Insurance

A percentage of wages with a small weekly cap.

Hawaii requires employers to provide Temporary Disability Insurance (TDI). The employee may be charged part of the cost through a payroll deduction that is at most 0.5% of wages, limited to a weekly maximum. The engine models that statutory ceiling, not a guaranteed exact figure, because the real employee share depends on the cost share the employer elects.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in Hawaii

The engine returns $15.00 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 0.50%, capped at $7.50/week ($15.00 this 2.00-week period)

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
Hawaii Disability Benefits (Employee)$3,000.00$15.00

Who pays

The employee pays up to the ceiling; the employer pays the rest of the cost, in a split the employer elects.

Points to remember

  • The cap is per week, so a biweekly check has twice the weekly cap.
  • Hawaii has state income tax with its own tables (HI_SIT).
  • Hawaii employers also pay unemployment, employment and training, and tech surcharge contributions; the response lists them as employer lines.
  • Hawaii also has prepaid health care requirements, which are an employer matter.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to HI, as HI_DBL_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See Hawaii payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

How is Hawaii TDI withheld?

As a percentage of wages with a weekly dollar cap for the employee share, deducted from pay.

Does the employer also pay something for Hawaii TDI?

Yes. The employer pays the part of the cost the employee is not charged.
