Payroll tax API for SaaS platforms

Add gross-to-net to your product without becoming a tax-rules company.

A vertical SaaS product (field service, restaurants, gig work, property management, staffing) often ends up needing payroll-shaped features: a net pay preview, a labour cost estimate, a pay stub. Building the tax rules to support them pulls a team away from the product. A tax calculation API covers the rules, and you keep the experience.

What platforms use it for

  • Net pay preview before a worker accepts a shift or a rate.
  • Pay stubs and statements that show each tax line with its name.
  • Labour cost that includes employer taxes, not just wages.
  • What-if comparisons for a raise or a move, with the reason for the difference.

Why it fits embedded use

  • Stateless. You send year-to-date wages; nothing is stored on our side that you would have to reconcile.
  • Per-calculation pricing. $0.08 per paycheck calculation, so cost follows usage and there is no seat or platform fee to pass through.
  • Published coverage. Your support team can look up what is covered on the coverage page instead of asking us.
  • Explainable lines. Show the reason beside the number so users do not file a ticket to ask.

An example you can show a product manager

A worker earns $3,000.00 biweekly in Texas. The platform shows net pay of $2,450.12 and the employer cost of $3,328.50 (gross plus $328.50 of employer taxes). Change the state to California and the preview reads $2,277.06.

What stays with you

The API calculates tax. Paying workers, filing returns and depositing tax are not part of it, so a platform that does those needs a payments or payroll provider as well. See embedding a payroll tax engine.

Yes. Omnia.tax is built for it: stateless calls, JSON in cents, per-calculation pricing and published coverage. It calculates tax only; it does not pay workers or file returns.
