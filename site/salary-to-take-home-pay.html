Salary to take-home pay

An annual salary is not a paycheck. The tax engine turns one into the other.

To find take-home pay from a yearly salary, divide the salary by the number of pay periods to get the gross per check, then run the check through the tax rules. Skipping the second step is the common mistake: a rule of thumb like "take-home is about 75%" is wrong by hundreds of dollars a year in either direction depending on the state.

Make it your own

  • Change the filing status and the W-4 entries: they change federal withholding.
  • Add pre-tax deductions: a 401(k) deferral or health premium raises take-home relative to what it costs.
  • Change the work address: local tax follows it.

Estimates

Take-home pay calculated this way is an estimate of withholding for a typical year. Actual withholding depends on the employee's W-4 and the employer's choices, and the final tax is settled on the return.

Questions

How do I convert an annual salary to take-home pay?

Divide the salary by the pay periods to get gross per check, then apply federal, state and local taxes and any deductions to reach net pay. A tax engine does the second step.

Is take-home pay a fixed percentage of salary?

No. It depends on the state, filing status, deductions and pay frequency, so a single percentage is wrong for most people.
