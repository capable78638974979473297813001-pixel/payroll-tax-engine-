Payroll tax API for payroll startups

The rules are not your differentiator. Spend the time elsewhere.

A new payroll product has a long list of things to build: onboarding, pay runs, payments, filings, support. Tax rules are the longest line and the least visible to a customer when they are right. A calculation API lets a startup ship a correct gross-to-net early and spend its first year on the parts customers choose it for.

What to buy

  • Federal, state and local withholding by check date.
  • Employer taxes: FUTA, state unemployment and state paid-leave contributions.
  • Address-to-jurisdiction resolution for local tax.
  • Garnishment limits and minimum-wage checks on the same call.

What you still own

  • Employee records, pay schedules and approvals.
  • Moving money: employee payments and tax deposits.
  • Filing returns and year-end forms.
  • Registration with state agencies and notices.

A staged build

StageWork
1Call the API for a gross-to-net preview on a manual pay run.
2Store the results as a pay stub and keep year-to-date totals.
3Add address resolution at onboarding.
4Add payments and deposits through partners.
5Add filings.

Stage 1 alone makes a credible demo: a $3,000.00 biweekly check in Columbus, Ohio nets $2,299.35 with every line named.

The honest comparison

Building the rules yourself is a real option and sometimes the right one. The cost is not the first version; it is the permanent upkeep. See build or buy.

Questions

Should a payroll startup build its own tax engine?

Usually not first. The first version is straightforward; the permanent cost is keeping every state and local rule current and effective-dated. Many startups buy the rules and build the experience.

Does the API handle filing?

No. Omnia.tax calculates withholding and employer taxes only. Filing and deposits are separate work you build or buy elsewhere.
