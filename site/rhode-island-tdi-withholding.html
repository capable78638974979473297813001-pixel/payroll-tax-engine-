Rhode Island Temporary Disability Insurance

TDI and Temporary Caregiver Insurance, paid by the employee.

Rhode Island funds Temporary Disability Insurance and Temporary Caregiver Insurance through an employee payroll deduction on wages up to a yearly cap. The employer withholds it and pays it over.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in Rhode Island

The engine returns $33.00 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 1.10%, capped at $100000.00/yr ($0.00 YTD already counted)

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
Rhode Island Disability Benefits (Employee)$3,000.00$33.00

Near the yearly limit the contribution stops. With $99,000.00 already counted this year against a $100,000.00 limit, only $1,000.00 of the same $3,000.00 check is left to charge, and the line becomes $11.00 (against $33.00 early in the year). The engine explains it: $1000.00 @ 1.10%, capped at $100000.00/yr ($99000.00 YTD already counted)

Who pays

The employee. There is no employer share of this contribution.

Points to remember

  • The wage cap is set each year by the state.
  • Track year-to-date in ytd.stateDisabilityEmployee.RI.
  • It is separate from Rhode Island income tax (RI_SIT).
  • Rhode Island is one of the few states with a state disability program funded by employees.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to RI, as RI_DBL_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See Rhode Island payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

Who pays Rhode Island TDI?

The employee, through payroll deduction, up to the yearly wage cap.

Is TDI the same as unemployment insurance?

No. Unemployment insurance is an employer tax. TDI is a separate employee-funded program for temporary disability.
