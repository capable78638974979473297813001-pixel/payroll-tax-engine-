Flat rate vs aggregate bonus withholding

Same bonus, two methods, different paychecks.

When a bonus is paid with regular pay, an employer can withhold on it at a flat federal rate or add it to the regular wages and withhold on the total. Which one withholds more depends on the size of the bonus and the employee's regular pay. Here are three bonus sizes on a $3,000.00 biweekly paycheck, run through the engine.

Three bonus sizes

What the pattern is

Aggregate withholding annualises the combined check, so a large bonus makes the engine treat the employee as if they earn that much every pay period. That pushes it into higher brackets for that one check. The flat rate stays at 22% regardless. For a small bonus the two are close. For a large bonus on modest regular pay, aggregation usually withholds more.

Which to choose

  • Flat rate is simple and predictable, and employees can see the number before the check. It requires the bonus to be identified separately from regular wages.
  • Aggregate follows the employee's W-4 more closely and can over-withhold on one large check.
  • Employers usually choose one policy and apply it consistently, but the rules allow the choice.

The same check, in the API

The only difference between the two requests is one field: the bonus earning's category, supplemental or regular.

Questions

Does the flat rate or the aggregate method withhold more?

It depends. Aggregation annualises the combined check, so a large bonus on modest regular pay usually withholds more than the 22% flat rate. For a small bonus the two are close.

Can I switch between methods?

The rules allow an employer to use either. Most apply one policy consistently, and the engine uses the method implied by how you categorise the earning.
