Withholding and wages

W-4 extra withholding (Step 4c)

A flat amount on top of whatever the percentage method says.

Line 4(c) of the Form W-4 lets an employee ask for an extra fixed amount to be withheld each pay period. It is the simplest way to fix an expected balance due: the employer calculates federal tax as usual, then adds the amount.

One example

Adding $50.00 on the $3,000.00 check changes federal withholding from $320.38 to $370.38, exactly $50.00 more, and net pay from $2,450.12 to $2,400.12. The line's detail ends: plus extra $50.00

How it differs from the other entries

EntryEffect
Step 2, multiple jobsChanges the bracket schedule.
Step 3, dependentsSubtracts an annual credit, spread over pay periods.
Step 4(a), other incomeAdds annual income before the percentage method.
Step 4(b), deductionsSubtracts annual deductions before the percentage method.
Step 4(c), extra withholdingAdds a flat amount to each check.

In the request

"federalW4": { "filingStatus": "single", "multipleJobs": false,
  "dependentCredit": 0, "otherIncome": 0, "deductions": 0,
  "extraWithholding": 5000 }   // per paycheck, in cents

Remember

  • It is per pay period, not per year.
  • It stays until the employee files a new Form W-4.
  • It applies to federal withholding only. State extra withholding is a separate field on the state certificate where the state offers one.

Questions

What is line 4(c) on the Form W-4?

Extra withholding: a fixed dollar amount the employee asks the employer to withhold from every paycheck, on top of the amount calculated from the rest of the form.

Does extra withholding carry to state tax?

No. It applies to federal income tax. Some states have their own extra-withholding entry on the state certificate.
