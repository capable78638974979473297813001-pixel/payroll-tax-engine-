Federal income tax withholding calculation

The percentage method behind a W-4 paycheck, with a real example.

Federal withholding is worked out per paycheck from the employee's Form W-4 and the IRS percentage method. The steps below are what the engine does for the 2020-or-later form.

The steps

  1. Taxable wages for the check: gross pay less the pre-tax deductions that reduce income tax wages.
  2. Annualise: multiply by the number of pay periods in the year.
  3. Adjust: add other income (step 4a), subtract deductions (step 4b), and apply the filing-status adjustment. The multiple-jobs box (step 2) selects the higher-withholding table.
  4. Find the bracket in the table for the filing status and compute the annual tentative tax.
  5. Subtract credits: the dependent amount from step 3.
  6. Divide back to one paycheck, then add extra withholding from step 4(c).

A worked paycheck

A single employee paid $3,000.00 biweekly, no W-4 adjustments. The engine's explanation of its federal income tax line:

annualised $78000.00 → adjusted $69400.00; bracket 22% over $57900.00; tentative/yr $8330.00 ÷ 26

That is $320.38 withheld on $3,000.00 of wages. Change one W-4 entry and the line changes with it.

Special cases

  • Exempt: an employee who claims exemption has no federal income tax withheld; Social Security and Medicare still apply.
  • Pre-2020 Form W-4: an employee who never filed the newer form is calculated from the old allowance method.
  • Bonuses: may use a flat supplemental rate; see bonus withholding.
  • Pension and annuity payments use a different form and are not modelled by this engine.

Questions

How is federal income tax withholding calculated?

From the Form W-4 using the IRS percentage method: annualise the check, adjust for filing status and the W-4 entries, find the bracket, subtract credits, then divide back to one paycheck and add any extra withholding.

What does the multiple-jobs checkbox do?

It selects the higher-withholding table, because each job is withheld as if it were the only one. It is on the Form W-4 as step 2.

Does this cover pensions?

No. Pension and annuity withholding use a different form and are not modelled. The engine assumes ordinary wage and salary payroll.
