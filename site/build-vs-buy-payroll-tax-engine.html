Buying and building

Build vs buy a payroll tax engine

The arithmetic is easy to build. The permanent cost is keeping the rules right.

Building a first version of payroll tax calculation takes weeks: Social Security, Medicare, a federal percentage method, a flat state rate. The trouble is everything after that.

Where the cost really is

  • Upkeep. Rates, brackets, wage bases and forms change every year and some mid-year. There are 51 state rule sets, each with its own method, and 4,141 local jurisdictions in just 15 of them.
  • Effective dating. A correction for an earlier year must use that year's rules, so old rules cannot be overwritten.
  • Per-tax wages. One deduction touches each tax differently, state by state. See pre-tax deductions.
  • Edge cases. Multi-state employees, reciprocity, supplemental wages, worker categories, caps carried across paychecks.
  • Proof. A payroll specialist has to be able to audit a line, so each one needs an explanation.

Build when

Payroll tax is your product, you have specialists to maintain it, and no vendor covers a jurisdiction you need.

Buy when

Payroll tax is a feature of something else, or you need to ship before a rules team could exist. Check what the vendor covers before you decide: ours is 15 states of local taxes on top of all states, and it does not file, deposit or run payroll.

A checklist for either

  • Can it reproduce a paycheck from last year, with last year's rules?
  • Does it carry year-to-date wages for the caps?
  • Does every line say how it was reached?
  • What happens when it cannot compute: an error that names the missing input, or a silent default?

Questions

Should I build my own payroll tax calculation?

Only if payroll tax is your product and you can staff the upkeep. The arithmetic is simple; the permanent cost is keeping every state and local rule current, effective-dated and tested.

What makes payroll tax hard to build?

Volume and change: dozens of state methods, thousands of local jurisdictions, yearly and mid-year updates, effective dating for corrections, and per-tax rules for deductions and caps.
