Payroll tax API for staffing agencies

Quote pay and cost accurately for workers placed in many states.

Staffing firms place people across states and cities, often in the same week. Two numbers drive each placement: what the worker takes home, and what the worker costs the agency once employer taxes are added. Both change with the assignment location, so a single rule of thumb is wrong more often than it is right.

Quote by assignment location

Run the same pay through different work locations. At $3,000.00 biweekly the engine returns employer taxes of $328.50 in Texas, $349.50 in California and $333.00 in Columbus, Ohio. The spread comes from state unemployment tax and, in some states, employer-paid leave contributions.

What a placement needs

  • The work address, found with POST /v1/address, so local tax is applied where the work happens.
  • The home address when it differs, for reciprocity and residence-based tax. See multi-state calculation.
  • The worker's W-4 and state certificate.
  • Year-to-date wages from your payroll, so caps stop at the right check.

Costing a bill rate

A worker at $37.50 an hour for 80 hours earns $3,000.00 gross. In Columbus the employer taxes on that check are $333.00, so the employer cost before insurance and overhead is $3,333.00, and net pay to the worker is $2,299.35. A bill rate has to cover the higher figure.

Limits

The API calculates. Workers' compensation, benefits cost, filing and deposits are outside it, and a staffing agency still needs a process or a provider for those.

Questions

How do staffing agencies use a payroll tax API?

To quote a worker's take-home and the agency's employer-tax cost for an assignment location, to apply local tax where the work is done, and to compare placements in different states.

Does it handle workers who live in a different state?

Yes. Send the work state and the residence state; the engine applies reciprocity and residence-based rules where they exist.
