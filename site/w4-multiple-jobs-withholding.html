Withholding and wages

W-4 multiple jobs withholding (Step 2)

One checkbox that changes which table the paycheck uses.

Step 2 of the Form W-4 is for employees with more than one job, or a spouse who works. Ticking the box tells the employer to withhold at a higher rate so that the combined jobs do not under-withhold. In the percentage method it switches the employer to a different bracket schedule. The effect on one paycheck is large.

The same pay, box ticked and not

Federal income taxNet pay
Box not ticked$320.38$2,450.12
Box ticked$503.35$2,267.15

On $3,000.00 biweekly for a single filer, ticking Step 2 raises federal withholding from $320.38 to $503.35, a difference of $182.97 a check. The engine's explanation for the ticked case: annualised $78000.00 → adjusted $78000.00; bracket 24% over $60900.00; tentative/yr $13087.00 ÷ 26

Why it withholds more

Without the box, each employer withholds as if their job were the only income, using the full standard deduction and low brackets. With two jobs that happens twice, and together they under-withhold. The higher-withholding schedule halves the benefit of the standard deduction and brackets to compensate.

In the request

"federalW4": { "filingStatus": "single", "multipleJobs": true,
  "dependentCredit": 0, "otherIncome": 0, "deductions": 0, "extraWithholding": 0 }

Notes

  • It applies to the 2020-or-later Form W-4. Employees on an older form follow different rules, which the engine also carries.
  • The checkbox is one way to complete Step 2; the worksheet or estimator on the form is another, and the result goes into other entries.
  • It affects withholding only. The employee's tax is settled on their return.

Questions

What does checking the multiple jobs box on the W-4 do?

It switches the employer to a higher-withholding bracket schedule so that two jobs together do not under-withhold. On a single filer paid $3,000 biweekly, it raises federal withholding noticeably each check.

Does it change Social Security or Medicare?

No. It affects federal income tax withholding only.
