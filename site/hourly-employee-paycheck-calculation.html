Hourly employee paycheck calculation

Hours and rate in. Net pay and every tax out.

An hourly paycheck starts with arithmetic you can do on a calculator: hours times rate, plus overtime. Everything after that is tax rules. The useful way to automate it is to compute the gross yourself, as an earning in cents, and let the engine do the rest.

A worked example

An employee in Columbus, Ohio works 80 hours at $25.00 an hour. Gross is 80 × $25.00 = $2,000.00.

Net pay is $1,594.98. The employer's lines ($222.00) are a cost on top of the gross.

Steps

  • Gross: hours × rate for regular time, and the overtime premium for hours over the threshold, in integer cents.
  • Send the earnings. Use one regular earning, or separate ones for regular and overtime.
  • Send deductions with their categories.
  • Send the W-4 and the work location. Local tax depends on the address.
  • Read the lines back and display them.

Two traps

  • Rounding gross to dollars before sending. Keep cents all the way: 37.5 hours at $18.75 is $703.125, and your payroll policy decides how it rounds to a cent.
  • Using the wrong pay frequency. A weekly check and a biweekly check of the same size withhold differently.

Questions

How do I calculate an hourly paycheck?

Multiply hours by the hourly rate, add overtime pay, send that as gross earnings in cents with deductions, the W-4 and the work location, and the engine returns each tax and net pay.

Does the API check minimum wage?

Yes. Add a minimumWageCheck with the hourly rate and the same call compares it with the federal, state and local minimum wage.
