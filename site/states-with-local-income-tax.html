Local taxes

States with local income and wage taxes

4,141 jurisdictions in 15 states in the Omnia.tax data.

Local income tax is the part of payroll tax most engines treat as an afterthought, and the part most likely to be wrong. A city, county or school district can tax wages by where the employee works, where they live or both. The engine carries 4,141 such jurisdictions in 15 states and publishes the list.

Where local tax exists

The coverage page lists every state and whether its local layer is listed in full or partial, with a count of jurisdictions. Highlights of the structures:

  • Ohio: municipal income tax, plus school district income taxes, found by address.
  • Pennsylvania: earned income tax by taxing district, plus the local services tax.
  • Michigan: 24 cities with their own resident and nonresident rates.
  • Kentucky: occupational license taxes by city, county and school district.
  • Indiana: county income taxes.
  • Maryland: county rates built into state withholding.
  • New York: New York City and Yonkers resident taxes.
  • Alabama and West Virginia: partial coverage, with the gaps stated.

Finding the right one

The address decides. POST /v1/address takes a work address and a home address and returns the state, the local jurisdictions and the certificate fields a paycheck needs. See address tax jurisdiction lookup.

Partial is stated, not hidden

Where a state's local data is incomplete the response says so and the coverage page lists which places are not covered. A rate that rests on a survey instead of the town's ordinance appears as a notice on the line. See data quality notices.

Questions

Which states have local income taxes?

In the Omnia.tax data, 15 states, including Ohio, Pennsylvania, Michigan, Kentucky, Indiana, Maryland, New York, Alabama and West Virginia. The coverage page lists each.

How does the engine know which local tax applies?

From the work and home addresses. The address endpoint returns the jurisdictions and the certificate fields for the paycheck call.
