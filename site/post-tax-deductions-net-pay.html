Post-tax deductions and net pay

They reduce take-home pay. They never reduce a tax.

A post-tax deduction comes out of pay after taxes are calculated. Examples are Roth retirement contributions, union dues, charitable giving through payroll, and after-tax insurance. It reduces net pay by the full amount and changes none of the tax lines. In the API it is a deduction with a null category.

Proof on one paycheck

The $3,000.00 check with a $300.00 post-tax deduction:

No deductionPost-tax deduction
Federal income tax$320.38$320.38
Social Security$186.00$186.00
Medicare$43.50$43.50
Net pay$2,450.12$2,150.12

Every tax line is identical. Net pay is lower by exactly $300.00, the full deduction.

Common post-tax deductions

  • Roth 401(k) or Roth 403(b) contributions
  • Union dues
  • Charitable contributions
  • After-tax life or disability insurance
  • Loan repayments and wage advances

Garnishments are also taken from pay after tax, but the amount is limited by law. See garnishment limits.

Order of operations

Gross pay, less pre-tax deductions, gives the base for each tax. Employee taxes come out. Post-tax deductions come out of what is left, leaving net pay. The identity is netPay = grossPay − pretaxDeductions − employeeTaxTotal − posttaxDeductions. See the paycheck formula.

Questions

Do post-tax deductions reduce taxes?

No. They are taken after taxes are calculated and change no tax line, only net pay.

Is a Roth 401(k) contribution pre-tax?

No. Roth contributions are made after tax, so they are post-tax deductions.
