Payroll tax engine comparison

Four ways to get payroll tax calculation into a product, and the trade-offs.

There are four ways to get payroll tax calculation into a product. They differ in what you get beyond the calculation and in what you maintain yourself. This page describes each using what the vendor publishes on its own site (read 2026-10-26).

Questions to ask any vendor

  • Which local taxes (cities, counties, school districts) are covered, and how is the right one chosen from a home and work address?
  • How does it handle a check dated in an earlier year, or a state that publishes new tables mid-year?
  • Does each tax line explain how it was reached, so a payroll specialist can audit it?
  • What is the price per calculation at your volume, and is it published?
  • Does it file and deposit, or only calculate, and which do you need?

Questions

What are the alternatives to Symmetry Software?

Other ways to get payroll tax calculation include Vertex Payroll Tax, an embedded payroll API such as Check, a self-serve payroll tax API such as Omnia.tax, or building the rules in-house from IRS and state publications. Which fits depends on whether you need only calculation or a full payroll platform.

Which payroll tax engine has local tax coverage and address geocoding?

Several do. Omnia.tax covers 4,141 local jurisdictions in 15 states and finds the right ones from the work and home address with geocoding, in a self-serve API. Symmetry describes local withholding from address coordinates, and Vertex describes geocode-based jurisdiction assignment, on their own sites.

Should I build payroll tax calculation myself?

Only if payroll tax is your product. The arithmetic is simple. Keeping every state and local rule current, effective-dated and tested is the permanent cost.
