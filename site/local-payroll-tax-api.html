Local payroll tax API

Cities, counties and school districts tax wages in some states. Which one applies depends on two addresses.

Beyond federal and state tax, some places tax wages locally: municipal income tax in Ohio, earned income tax and local services tax in Pennsylvania, school-district income tax in Ohio and Kentucky, occupational taxes in Kentucky and Alabama, and others. The local layer is the most common reason a payroll tax result is wrong.

What Omnia.tax covers

4,141 local jurisdictions across 15 states, plus 69 local minimum wage ordinances. The full list, by state and by jurisdiction, is on the coverage page, including which states are listed in full and which only in part.

How the right jurisdiction is chosen

Local tax usually follows the work location, the home location, or both. A street address is resolved to the city, county, school district and the state's own codes, which become the workState.certificate on the paycheck request. The address endpoint does this, and shows what it could and could not resolve.

What it says it does not know

Where a state's local layer is only partly listed, the coverage page says so, and a lookup that cannot determine a jurisdiction says which ones it could not check. We would rather report a gap than guess a rate.

Questions

Which states have local payroll taxes?

Omnia.tax carries local taxes in 15 states, 4,141 jurisdictions in all. The coverage page lists them by state and says where a state's local layer is only partly listed.

How does the API know which city tax applies?

It resolves the work address, and the home address when it differs, to a city, county and school district, and the paycheck calculation applies the local taxes for those places.
