Buying and building

Payroll tax data sources

Every rate in the engine is traceable to a document.

A tax engine is only as good as the documents behind its numbers. The Omnia.tax rule files cite their sources inline, next to the number they support, with the date each was read. The 2026 state rule files alone carry 334 cited sources pointing to 305 distinct web addresses, counted by reading the files, not estimated.

What a source entry holds

  • The title of the document, as the agency names it.
  • The URL it was read from.
  • The date it was verified.
  • Where the figure is a quotation, the quotation.

Primary first

The preference order is the levying body's own document: the state revenue department's withholding guide, the city's ordinance, the agency's rate notice. A rate that rests on something weaker, such as a municipal league survey or a secondary summary, is marked so, and the response carries a notice on the affected line. See data quality notices.

When sources conflict

Where two sources disagree, the file records both and the engine flags the line as conflicting rather than silently choosing. The Alabama municipal data is an example, with tiers for primary source, survey only, conflicting and not modelled.

What is not in a source

  • Interpretations are the engine's. The comments in the rule files say where a rule was inferred.
  • Years not yet final are marked provisional in the response.
  • Anything the engine does not cover is listed on the coverage page.

Check us

Every state page links the withholding guide its rule file was read from. Confirm any figure against the agency before relying on it for filing.

Questions

Where do the tax rates come from?

From the levying agency's own publications where available, cited in the rule files with the date they were read. Weaker sources are marked and flagged on the response.

What happens when two sources disagree?

The rule file records both, and the response flags the line as a conflicting-source notice instead of choosing silently.
