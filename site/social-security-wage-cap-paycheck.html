Withholding and wages

Social Security wage cap on a paycheck

The tax is 6.2% up to $184,500 in 2026, and then it stops.

Social Security tax applies to wages up to a yearly limit, the wage base: $184,500 for 2026 and $192,300 for 2027. Each of the employee and employer pays 6.2%. Past the limit the tax is zero, so net pay rises at the check where the cap is reached.

The check that straddles the cap

An employee paid $10,000.00 per check has $180,500.00 counted so far. Only $4,000.00 remains under the $184,500.00 base.

Why the engine needs the year to date

The only way to know the cap was reached is the Social Security wages already paid this year. Send them in ytd.socialSecurity. See year-to-date wages.

What counts toward the cap

  • Wages subject to Social Security tax, which excludes pre-tax Section 125 premiums but includes 401(k) deferrals.
  • Only wages paid by this employer count, unless a successor-employer rule says otherwise.
  • Medicare has no cap, so it keeps going: see Medicare tax calculation.

Questions

What is the Social Security wage base for 2026?

$184,500. Employee and employer each pay 6.2% on wages up to that amount.

Why does take-home pay jump near the end of the year for high earners?

Once wages reach the wage base, Social Security tax stops, so later checks have no Social Security withholding.
