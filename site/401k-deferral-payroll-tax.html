Withholding and wages

401(k) deferrals and payroll tax

Pre-tax for income tax. Not pre-tax for FICA.

A traditional 401(k) deferral lowers the wages used for federal and most state income tax. It does not lower wages for Social Security or Medicare, which is a common source of confusion on a pay stub. The 2026 elective-deferral limit is $24,500 for 401(k), 403(b) and governmental 457(b) plans combined under section 402(g).

The $300.00 deferral cuts federal withholding by $66.00. Net pay falls by only $234.00, less than the deferral itself, because part of it is offset by lower tax. Social Security stays at $186.00.

Roth is different

A Roth 401(k) contribution is made after tax, so it is a post-tax deduction: send it with a null category. It reduces net pay by the full amount and changes no tax line. Run it and the federal tax is $320.38, the same as with no deduction.

Questions

Does a 401(k) deferral reduce Social Security tax?

No. It reduces federal and most state income tax wages but remains subject to Social Security and Medicare.

How is a Roth 401(k) contribution handled?

It is after-tax, so it is a post-tax deduction. It lowers net pay by the full amount and does not change any tax line.
