W-4 filing status and withholding

Three filing statuses, three different amounts on the same pay.

The filing status in Step 1 of the Form W-4 chooses the standard-deduction adjustment and the bracket table used to withhold. The same wages produce different federal income tax for each status.

The same pay, three statuses

Filing statusFederal income taxNet pay
Single or married filing separately$320.38$2,450.12
Married filing jointly$192.31$2,578.19
Head of household$234.92$2,535.58

All three use $3,000.00 paid biweekly. The married-filing-jointly employee has $128.07 less withheld each check than the single employee.

Why they differ

Each status carries a different standard deduction and wider or narrower brackets. The engine's explanation for married filing jointly: annualised $78000.00 → adjusted $65100.00; bracket 12% over $44100.00; tentative/yr $5000.00 ÷ 26 Compare with single: annualised $78000.00 → adjusted $69400.00; bracket 22% over $57900.00; tentative/yr $8330.00 ÷ 26

Questions

How does filing status change withholding?

It selects the standard-deduction adjustment and the bracket table. Married filing jointly withholds less than single on the same wages because the deduction is larger and the brackets are wider.

Does filing status change FICA?

No. Social Security and Medicare are the same for every filing status.
