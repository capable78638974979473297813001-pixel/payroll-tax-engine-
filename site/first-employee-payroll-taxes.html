Payroll taxes on your first employee

A first paycheck, line by line.

Hiring your first employee turns you into a withholding agent. You withhold some tax from their pay, pay some of your own on top, and hand both to the government on a schedule. The amounts are easier to understand from one example than from a list of rules.

The employee is single, paid $3,000.00 biweekly in Texas. They take home $2,450.12. You owe $328.50 on top: Social Security (Employer) $186.00, Medicare (Employer) $43.50, FUTA $18.00, Texas Unemployment Insurance (Employer) $81.00.

The employer side

Employer Social Security and Medicare each match the employee's. FUTA is $18.00 on this check at the start of the year because it applies to the first $7,000 of each employee's pay. State unemployment tax is the largest early cost in most states; see wage bases by state.

Then what

  • Deposit withheld and employer tax on the schedule the IRS and your state assign.
  • File quarterly and annual returns and issue a Form W-2 after year end.
  • Keep payroll records for the periods the law requires.

The calculator does none of these. A payroll service does, and the calculator checks its work.

Questions

What taxes do I withhold from my first employee?

Federal income tax from their Form W-4, the employee share of Social Security and Medicare, and state and local income tax where they apply, plus any state employee contributions.

What do I pay on top?

The employer match of Social Security and Medicare, federal unemployment tax, state unemployment tax, and in some states paid-leave contributions.
