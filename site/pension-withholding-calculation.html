Pension and annuity withholding calculation

Pension payments are not wages. They are withheld on Form W-4P and their own worksheet, and carry no Social Security or Medicare tax.

A payer of periodic pension, annuity, profit-sharing, stock-bonus or IRA payments withholds federal income tax from the taxable part of each payment, using the payee's Form W-4P. The method is Publication 15-T's Worksheet 1B, which looks like the wage worksheet and differs from it in four places.

How it differs from wage withholding

  • No FICA, FUTA or unemployment tax. The only tax on the payment is federal income tax.
  • The standard adjustment always applies. Annual payments are reduced by $8,600 ($12,900 if married filing jointly) with no multiple-jobs exception; the Form W-4P has Step 2 for other jobs and pensions instead.
  • Credits come off the annual tax first. The Step 3 credit is subtracted from the annual tentative tax before it is divided into payments.
  • With no form, the payee is Single with no entries. There is no "Married" default for payees whose payments began after 2021.

The steps in Worksheet 1B

  1. Annualise the payment (monthly ×12, semimonthly ×24, biweekly ×26, weekly ×52, daily ×260).
  2. Add Step 4(a) other income; subtract Step 4(b) deductions and the standard adjustment. This can be negative.
  3. If Step 2(b)(iii) has an amount, work out the extra tax the other jobs and pensions call for (Parts I to III); otherwise look up the annual tax on the standard schedule for the filing status.
  4. Subtract the Step 3 credits, never below zero.
  5. Divide by the number of payments, and add Step 4(c) extra withholding.

Checked against the IRS's own tables

The result for a payee with no form equals the tax on annual payments above the standard deduction, from the 2026 Marginal Rate Tables printed on Form W-4R, divided by the number of payments. We test the engine against those tables across three filing statuses and four payment frequencies.

No federalW4 or ytd is needed. The response has a federal line, US_FIT_PENSION, and, for the payee's state, a line such as NC_PENSION_SIT: see state pension withholding.

What it does not do

  • Local withholding on pension payments is not calculated, and neither is state withholding for a state we have not checked against its revenue agency's guidance: see state pension withholding. The response says so.
  • Quarterly, semiannual and annual periodic payments are refused: Pub 15-T's table lists monthly, semimonthly, biweekly, weekly and daily.
  • Nonresident aliens are outside these methods (Pub 515).
  • Lump sums and rollovers use Form W-4R: see eligible rollover and nonperiodic withholding.

Questions

How is withholding on a pension calculated?

For periodic payments, from the payee's Form W-4P using Pub 15-T Worksheet 1B: annualise the payment, apply the standard adjustment and the form's entries, find the annual tax on the standard schedule, subtract credits, and divide by the number of payments.

What if a retiree gave the payer no Form W-4P?

The payer withholds as if the payee were Single with no entries in Steps 2 to 4. For payments that began before 2022 with no form, the payee is treated as married claiming three allowances, under the older rules.

Is Social Security or Medicare tax withheld from a pension?

No. FICA applies to wages. A pension payment has federal income tax withheld and, depending on the state, state income tax.

Does Omnia.tax calculate state tax on pensions?

For the states whose revenue agency publishes a rule we have read and checked, yes: send the payee's state and any election they made. For every other state it returns no state line and a warning that says so. Local withholding is not calculated.
