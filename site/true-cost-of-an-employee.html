True cost of an employee: employer payroll taxes

The salary is not the cost. The employer side adds more.

A salary understates what an employee costs. On top of gross pay the employer owes its share of Social Security and Medicare, federal unemployment tax, state unemployment tax, and in some states paid-leave contributions. The employer lines are in every response under payer: "employer".

Why the first check looks expensive

State unemployment tax and FUTA apply to the first part of each employee's yearly wages, up to a wage base (FUTA is 0.6% of the first $7,000 in most states; state bases run from $7,000 up). They hit early in the year and stop, so the first checks cost more than the last. Social Security stops at $184,500.

What this does not include

  • Workers' compensation, health benefits and other insurance.
  • Paid time off, equipment and overhead.
  • Quarterly employer taxes that apply to total payroll rather than to one check: New York MCTMT and the Nevada MBT, available from /v1/employer-taxes.

Questions

How much do employer payroll taxes add to an employee's cost?

Employer Social Security and Medicare add 7.65% of wages up to the Social Security wage base, and FUTA and state unemployment add more early in the year. The exact share depends on the state.

Do employer taxes come out of the employee's pay?

No. They are paid by the employer on top of gross pay and do not reduce net pay.
