2027 payroll tax changes by state

The figures that differ between the 2026 and 2027 rule files.

Payroll tax rules change every January. This page compares the 2026 and 2027 rule files the engine carries and lists what moves: the Social Security wage base, state unemployment wage bases, and flat income tax rates. Years that are not final carry a provisional notice in the response, so treat 2027 figures as the best available, not as settled.

How to use it

  • Run your year-end test paychecks with a 2027 check date to see the effect before January.
  • Check the response warnings for provisional-year notices.
  • Agency announcements after the date of the rule files may not be reflected; confirm with the state.

What the Social Security change means for one employee

The wage base rises from $184,500 to $192,300, so the most an employee pays in Social Security rises from $11,439.00 to $11,922.60, and the employer pays the same again. That is $483.60 more per high earner on each side. The change only reaches employees paid above the 2026 base, but it moves the point in the year where Social Security stops.
