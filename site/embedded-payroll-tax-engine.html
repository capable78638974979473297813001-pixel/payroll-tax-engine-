Buying and building

Embedded payroll tax engine

For teams building a payroll, HR or staffing product who want the tax math as a service.

If your product pays people, or shows what people are paid, it needs payroll tax math. Embedding an engine means your code sends a paycheck and renders the result, and someone else keeps the rules current.

What the engine covers

  • Federal, state and local income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare.
  • Employer taxes: federal and state unemployment, state paid-leave contributions, and quarterly employer taxes in New York and Nevada.
  • Garnishment limits and a minimum-wage check on the same call.
  • Address resolution so you can store an employee once.

What you still build

The employee and pay-run data model, approvals, paying employees, filing and depositing of the tax, and the screens. Omnia.tax calculates and does not do those.

Integration shape

  • One POST /v1/paycheck per employee per pay run; POST /v1/payCalc for batches.
  • A published OpenAPI document to generate a client from.
  • Field-level validation errors you can show to your users.
  • Idempotency keys so a retry is not counted twice.

Questions

What is an embedded payroll tax engine?

A tax calculation service you call from inside your own payroll or HR product, so you do not build and maintain the federal, state and local rules yourself.

Does Omnia.tax handle filing and deposits?

No. It calculates only. Filing returns, depositing tax and paying employees are separate services you provide or buy elsewhere.
