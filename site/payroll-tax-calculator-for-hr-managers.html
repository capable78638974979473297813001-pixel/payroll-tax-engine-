Who it is for

Payroll tax calculator for HR managers

Answer "what will I take home?" and "what does this cost us?" with one tool.

HR gets two kinds of money questions: from candidates and employees ("what will I take home?") and from finance ("what does this role cost?"). Both are payroll tax calculations, and doing them by rule of thumb gives answers that are wrong by different amounts in different states.

A relocation question

An employee on $3,000.00 biweekly asks what a move from Texas to California does to their check. Net pay would go from $2,450.12 to $2,277.06, a drop of $173.06 per check. The comparison shows which lines account for it.

An offer question

A candidate in Columbus, Ohio asks what $3,000.00 biweekly nets. Answer: $2,299.35, after federal income tax, Ohio tax, Columbus city tax, Social Security and Medicare. State the assumption (single, no deductions) and offer to rerun with their details.

A cost question

Finance asks what the role costs. The Columbus check costs you $3,333.00: gross plus $333.00 of employer taxes, before benefits and overhead.

Good practice

  • Say the assumptions: filing status, deductions, location.
  • Use an estimate language: withholding follows the employee's W-4, and the final tax is settled on the return.
  • Do not promise a number the engine did not return.
  • Keep personal data out of the tool; you do not need names to calculate.

Questions

Can HR share a take-home estimate with a candidate?

Yes, as an estimate. State the assumptions (filing status, deductions, work location), because the real withholding follows the employee's own W-4.

Does the calculator need personal data?

No. It needs pay, location and withholding choices, not a name or an identifier.
