New York DBL employee deduction

A small weekly-capped contribution next to Paid Family Leave.

New York's Disability Benefits Law (DBL) requires employers to provide short-term disability coverage. The employee may be charged part of the cost through a payroll deduction that is a percentage of wages with a small weekly dollar maximum.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in New York

The engine returns $1.20 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 0.50%, capped at $0.60/week ($1.20 this 2.00-week period) — cap applied

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
New York Disability Benefits (Employee)$3,000.00$1.20

Who pays

The employee, up to the weekly cap. The rest of the cost of coverage is the employer's.

Points to remember

  • The cap is per week, so a biweekly check has twice the weekly cap.
  • It is separate from Paid Family Leave (NY_PFML_EE) and from state income tax.
  • The engine states when the cap was applied: the detail reads "cap applied" on a check large enough to reach it.
  • Employers may not deduct more than the statutory maximum.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to NY, as NY_DBL_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See New York payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

What is the New York DBL deduction?

A percentage of wages, capped at a small weekly dollar amount, that the employee may be charged toward disability coverage required by the Disability Benefits Law.

Is DBL the same as Paid Family Leave?

No. They are separate programs with separate rates and caps, and appear as separate lines.
