States with disability insurance payroll deductions

Where an employee-side SDI or TDI line appears on the paycheck.

Most states do not have a state disability insurance program. A few do, and in those the employee usually pays through payroll. The engine returns a line for each. This list is produced by running a $3,000.00 biweekly check in every state and listing those that return a disability line, so it cannot drift from the rules.

5 states return an employee disability line on this check.

How they differ

  • California charges a percentage of all wages with no yearly cap; Rhode Island charges a percentage up to a yearly wage cap.
  • Some cap by the week at a small dollar amount (Hawaii, New York).
  • New Jersey combines disability with family leave and unemployment lines of its own.
  • In all of them the employer still owes unemployment tax.

Questions

Which states have state disability insurance?

California, Hawaii, New Jersey, New York and Rhode Island run state-mandated disability programs funded in whole or part through employee deductions. The table above comes from running a paycheck in each state.

Does the employer also pay?

In some of them, yes. Hawaii and New York share the cost, and New Jersey has an employer disability line. California and Rhode Island are employee-funded.
