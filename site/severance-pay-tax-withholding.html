Withholding and wages

Severance pay tax withholding

Severance is wages. It is taxed like a bonus, not like a gift.

Severance pay is wages for tax purposes. It is subject to federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare, and state tax where there is one. The usual treatment for federal income tax is the supplemental wage rules: 22% flat when paid separately, or aggregation, and 37% over $1,000,000 in the year.

Points that surprise people

  • Social Security and Medicare apply even if the person has left. Severance is wages for the year in which it is paid.
  • A lump sum can cross the Social Security wage base or the Additional Medicare threshold. Send year-to-date wages so the engine splits the check.
  • Some states have special treatment for severance or require specific withholding. Check the state page.
  • Severance is not exempt because it follows a layoff.

Not covered

Retirement-plan distributions have their own withholding rules; see eligible rollover withholding. Whether a payment is severance, a settlement or damages is a legal question the engine does not decide.

Questions

Is severance pay subject to payroll taxes?

Yes. Severance is wages: federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and state tax apply, with the supplemental wage rules available for federal withholding.

How is federal withholding calculated on severance?

Commonly at the 22% flat supplemental rate when paid separately, or by aggregating with regular wages, and at 37% on supplemental wages over $1,000,000 in the year.
