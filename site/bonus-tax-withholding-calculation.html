Bonus tax withholding calculation

Bonuses and commissions are supplemental wages. They can be withheld differently from regular pay.

Federal rules let an employer withhold on supplemental wages, such as bonuses and commissions, at a flat rate instead of running them through the regular percentage method. The engine treats an earning with the category supplemental that way, and states each decide their own treatment.

A worked example

A Texas employee, paid $3,000.00 of regular pay and a $5,000.00 bonus on the same biweekly check:

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
Federal Income Tax$3,000.00$320.38
Federal Income Tax (Supplemental)$5,000.00$1,100.00
Social Security$8,000.00$496.00
Medicare$8,000.00$116.00

The bonus is withheld at a flat rate: $5,000.00 of supplemental wages produces $1,100.00 of federal income tax ($5000.00 @ 22% flat supplemental rate). The regular $3,000.00 is taxed by the normal method. Social Security and Medicare apply to the whole $8,000.00.

Why the take-home feels low

The flat rate can be higher than the employee's normal marginal rate, so a bonus looks heavily taxed on the paycheck. Any over-withholding comes back when the employee files, but it is real money out of that check.

State treatment

Several states offer a flat supplemental rate as an employer option, and the engine only applies it when the request says the employer elected it; by default it aggregates. Some states aggregate a stand-alone bonus with the last regular payment, which needs that payment as an input.

Paying a bonus after tax

If the promise is a take-home amount, use net to gross to find the bonus that nets it.

Questions

How is a bonus taxed?

Federal withholding on a bonus can use a flat supplemental rate (22% for most amounts) instead of the regular percentage method. Social Security and Medicare apply as normal, and each state has its own treatment.

Why is tax on my bonus so high?

The flat withholding rate can exceed your normal marginal rate, so more is withheld from that check than will be owed. The difference is settled when you file.

Can an employer withhold a bonus at the regular rate?

Yes. Aggregating the bonus with regular pay and running the normal method is the alternative to the flat rate. The engine aggregates by default for states where the flat rate is an employer option.
