Withholding and wages

Additional Medicare tax withholding

The 0.9% employee-only tax that starts partway through the year.

Additional Medicare tax is 0.9% on an employee's Medicare wages above $200,000 in a calendar year. The employer withholds it once wages pass that figure, regardless of the employee's filing status, and does not match it. It is separate from regular Medicare, which is 1.45% on every dollar with no cap.

How the threshold works

The engine needs to know the Medicare wages already paid this year, in ytd.medicare. A $10,000.00 check with no earlier wages owes no Additional Medicare tax, since it does not reach $200,000. The same check after $195,000.00 has already been paid crosses the line by $5,000.00:

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
Medicare$10,000.00$145.00
Medicare (Employer)$10,000.00$145.00
Additional Medicare$5,000.00$45.00

Reading the example

Regular Medicare is $145.00 on the whole $10,000.00, and the employer pays a matching $145.00. Additional Medicare is $45.00, which is 0.9% of the $5,000.00 over the threshold. There is no employer share of that line.

Without year-to-date wages

Run the same $10,000.00 check with ytd.medicare at zero and the engine returns no Additional Medicare line ($0.00). If your system does not track year-to-date wages, it will under-withhold in the check that should have started the tax. See year-to-date wages.

What it is not

  • It is not a second Medicare match for the employer.
  • It does not depend on filing status for withholding, although the employee's actual liability on Form 8959 does.
  • It is not capped.

Questions

When does Additional Medicare tax start?

Employers begin withholding 0.9% on the part of an employee's Medicare wages that exceeds $200,000 in the calendar year, whatever the employee's filing status.

Does the employer match Additional Medicare tax?

No. It is paid by the employee only.
