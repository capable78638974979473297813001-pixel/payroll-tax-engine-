States and rates

Bonus withholding by state

The federal rule is one number. The states are not.

Federal withholding on a bonus can use a flat 22% rate. States set their own rules, and they differ more than the federal ones: a fixed supplemental rate in some, aggregation with regular pay in others, no tax in 9 jurisdictions. This list is read from the 2026 state rule files the engine uses.

16 states carry a flat supplemental rate in the rule files. California has two (a higher rate for bonuses and stock options, a lower one for other supplemental pay), so it is recorded separately and not in this list.

Everything else

  • No state income tax: Alaska, Florida, New Hampshire, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming.
  • Aggregation: in most other states a bonus is combined with regular pay and run through the state's normal method, which is what happens when you do not tag the earning supplemental.
  • Employer choice: some states allow either a flat rate or aggregation.

Caution

A rate in the data is the state's supplemental rate for withholding. States update these, and some limit when the flat rate may be used. Confirm against the state's current withholding guide before relying on a figure, and read the state page for the source.

Questions

Which states have a flat withholding rate for bonuses?

Several states publish a flat supplemental rate, listed in the table above from the 2026 rule files. Others require aggregation with regular pay, and some have no income tax.

Where is each state's source?

On the state's own page under /payroll-tax, which links to the withholding guide the rule file was read from.
