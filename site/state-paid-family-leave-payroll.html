States with paid family leave payroll contributions

Every state where the engine returns a paid-leave line, and which side pays.

State paid family and medical leave programs keep adding new payroll lines. Each state sets its own rate, wage cap and employer-employee split. This list comes from running a $3,000.00 biweekly check in every state and listing the paid-leave lines the engine returns.

10 jurisdictions return at least one paid-leave line on this check. California is not in the list because its State Disability Insurance contribution funds Paid Family Leave in one line: see California SDI.

Why it matters to an engine

  • The split between employee and employer differs by state, and sometimes by employer size.
  • Most have a yearly wage cap, so year-to-date wages must be sent: ytd.statePaidLeave.
  • Programs are added and rates change nearly every January, so the check date matters.
  • Washington has no income tax, so its paid-leave and long-term-care lines are most of the state-specific paycheck there.

Not shown

States whose programs depend on employer choices the engine cannot see, such as a private plan election or employer size, show the default treatment. The state's own page and the response notices say when that applies. Delaware, for example, funds its program from the employer with an optional recovery from employees, which is an employer election and not a fixed rate.

Questions

Which states have paid family leave contributions?

In the engine's data: Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Oregon and Washington, and the District of Columbia. California funds Paid Family Leave through its SDI contribution. The table above lists the lines the engine returns.

Do employers or employees pay?

It depends on the state. Some are employee-funded, some employer-funded and some shared. The table shows which side each line is on.
