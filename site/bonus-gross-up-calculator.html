Bonus gross-up calculator

Net to gross for a bonus: what to pay so they take home exactly the target.

Sometimes a bonus is promised as a take-home figure: "we will give you $2,500 after taxes". The gross that achieves it is not the target divided by one minus a tax rate, because the taxes are layered and have thresholds. The gross-up endpoint searches with the real engine until it finds the smallest amount that leaves the target.

A worked case

An employee in Columbus, Ohio earns $3,000.00 regular pay and is promised a bonus large enough that the whole check nets $4,500.00. The solver returns a bonus of $3,409.11, with net pay of $4,500.00 (exact).

In the request

POST /v1/paycheck/gross-up
{ …a paycheck request…,
  "grossUp": { "targetNetPay": 450000, "earningIndex": 1 } }

earningIndex says which earning to solve for. The amount you send there is ignored. The response has grossUp, request (your request with the solved amount, ready to reuse) and result.

When it cannot be exact

Some tax steps jump, so no whole-cent amount hits the target. The engine returns the smallest amount that reaches it, with exact: false and the cents over in overTarget. An imputed earning cannot raise take-home pay and returns cannot_gross_up.

A policy note

A gross-up is a decision about who bears the tax. It is also more expensive: the employer pays tax on the grossed-up amount. Run the employer side too, as in the true cost of an employee.

Questions

How do I gross up a bonus?

Use the gross-up endpoint with the paycheck, a target net pay and the index of the bonus earning. It finds the smallest bonus that leaves that take-home using the real engine.

Why is the answer not target ÷ (1 − rate)?

Taxes are layered with thresholds, caps and different bases, so no single rate applies. The solver uses the engine itself.
