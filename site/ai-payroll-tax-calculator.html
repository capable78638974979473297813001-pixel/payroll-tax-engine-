AI payroll tax calculator

Assistants are good at asking questions and bad at tax arithmetic. Pair them with an engine.

People ask AI assistants payroll questions all the time: what will this employee net, what does moving to another state cost, how much is the employer tax on this hire. A model answering from memory will sound confident and be off. An assistant that calls a tax engine gives the engine's number and can show its working.

The failure mode

A language model has no table of 2026 brackets in its head and cannot know that a particular Ohio municipality changed its rate. It produces a plausible figure. In payroll, plausible is the dangerous kind of wrong, because nobody checks a number that looks right.

The fix

Give the assistant a tool. Through the MCP server it can run calculate_paycheck for the real inputs and report the result. In the example below, the same Texas paycheck is asked two ways, and the engine's answer is the only one worth quoting.

QuestionEngine answer
Net pay on $3,000.00 biweekly in Texas, single filer$2,450.12
The same pay in California$2,277.06
Employer taxes on the Texas check$328.50

What stays human

  • Deciding the employee's W-4 answers. The assistant should ask, not assume.
  • Approving a payroll run. A calculation is not a decision to pay.
  • Filing and depositing. The engine does not, and neither does the assistant.

Good prompts for the connected assistant

  • "Compare this employee's paycheck in Austin and in Denver and explain the difference."
  • "What gross pay leaves $2,500 take-home in Columbus, Ohio?"
  • "Is there a notice on any line of this paycheck?"

Questions

Can an AI assistant calculate payroll taxes accurately?

Not from memory. It can if it calls a tax engine, because then the answer is the engine's result rather than an estimate.

How do I connect an assistant to Omnia.tax?

Point it at the POST /mcp endpoint with a bearer key. It speaks JSON-RPC 2.0 over streamable HTTP and exposes six tools.
