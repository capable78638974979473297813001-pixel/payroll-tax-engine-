Withholding and wages

Supplemental wage withholding rates

The federal flat rates for bonuses and similar pay, and when each applies.

Supplemental wages are pay on top of regular wages: bonuses, commissions, overtime pay paid separately, severance and similar payments. For federal income tax the employer has two ways to withhold on them. One is a flat rate. The other is to add the payment to regular wages and run the ordinary percentage method on the total. The rates below are read from the federal rule file the engine uses for 2026.

The rates

SituationFederal withholding
Supplemental wages identified separately, flat-rate method22% on the supplemental amount
Cumulative supplemental wages to one employee over $1,000,000 in the year37% on the part over $1,000,000, mandatory
Aggregate methodAdd to the regular payment and withhold on the total under the percentage method

A bonus, both ways

An employee paid $3,000.00 regular biweekly pay gets a $5,000.00 bonus in the same check. Flat rate: federal withholding on the bonus is $1,100.00 (22% of $5,000.00), plus $320.38 on the regular pay, $1,420.38 in total. Aggregate: the $8,000.00 is treated as one payment and federal withholding is $1,486.69. The difference is $66.31 in this check, which is withholding only; the employee's actual tax is settled on their return.

How to ask for each

Tag the earning category: "supplemental" for the flat method. Leave the bonus as regular to aggregate it. The engine does not guess which you intend, because the choice belongs to the employer and the rules allow either.

The $1 million line

Send the supplemental wages paid so far this year in ytd.supplemental. When a check crosses $1,000,000 the engine splits it. A $400,000.00 bonus on top of $800,000.00 already paid this year returns federal withholding of $118,000.00, and the line explains why: $200000.00 @ 22% + $200000.00 @ 37% (cumulative supplemental over $1000000.00)

State rules differ

States have their own supplemental treatment. Some use a flat rate, some require aggregation, some leave it to the employer. See bonus withholding by state.

Questions

What is the federal supplemental wage withholding rate?

Under the flat-rate method it is 22% on supplemental wages identified separately from regular wages. Cumulative supplemental wages over $1,000,000 in a year are withheld at a mandatory 37% on the excess.

Can an employer use the aggregate method instead?

Yes. The employer may add the supplemental pay to regular wages for the period and withhold on the total using the percentage method.

Is withholding the same as the tax owed on a bonus?

No. Withholding is an advance. The employee's actual tax on the bonus is settled on their return, so a refund or a balance due is possible either way.
