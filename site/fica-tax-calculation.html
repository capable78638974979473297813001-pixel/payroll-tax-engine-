FICA tax calculation

Social Security and Medicare, the cap, and what happens on the paycheck that crosses it.

FICA is two taxes, each paid half by the employee and half by the employer.

The rates in the 2026 and 2027 rules

TaxEmployeeEmployerCap
Social Security6.2%6.2%$184,500 (2026), $192,300 (2027)
Medicare1.45%1.45%None
Additional Medicare0.9%NoneOn wages above $200,000, withheld from the employee

The most Social Security tax one employee can pay in 2026 is $184,500 × 6.2% = $11,439.00, and the employer pays the same again.

The paycheck that crosses the cap

A Texas employee paid $10,000.00 on one check, with $180,000.00 of Social Security wages already paid this year. Only the wages below the cap are taxed:

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
Social Security$4,500.00$279.00
Social Security (Employer)$4,500.00$279.00
Medicare$10,000.00$145.00
Medicare (Employer)$10,000.00$145.00

Social Security applies to $4,500.00, the room left under the cap, while Medicare applies to the full $10,000.00. This is why year-to-date wages are an input to the calculation.

Which pay counts

Wages that count for FICA are not always the wages that count for income tax: a traditional 401(k) deferral reduces one and not the other. See pre-tax deductions by tax. Some worker categories (clergy, household, railroad, election workers) have their own FICA treatment.

Questions

What is the Social Security wage base for 2026?

$184,500. It is $192,300 for 2027. Social Security tax stops once an employee's wages for the year pass it.

What is the FICA tax rate?

Social Security is 6.2% from the employee and 6.2% from the employer; Medicare is 1.45% from each, with no cap. Employees also pay 0.9% Additional Medicare on wages above $200,000.

Does the employer pay FICA too?

Yes. The employer pays a matching share of Social Security and Medicare, which is a cost to the employer and not taken from the employee. The Additional Medicare Tax is paid by the employee only.
