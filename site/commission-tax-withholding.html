Withholding and wages

Commission tax withholding

Commissions are supplemental wages. Pick a method and be consistent.

Commissions are supplemental wages for federal withholding. An employer can withhold at the flat 22% rate when the commission is paid separately from regular pay, or aggregate it with regular wages. Social Security and Medicare apply to the whole amount either way.

A commission check

A sales employee has $3,000.00 of regular biweekly pay and a $2,500.00 commission paid in the same check.

MethodFederal withheldNet pay
Flat rate, commission identified separately$870.38$4,208.87
Aggregate with regular pay$886.69$4,192.56
Commission paid alone, flat rate$550.00$1,758.75

Choosing

  • If commissions are paid on a separate schedule, the flat rate is the usual choice and easy to explain.
  • If they are paid with regular wages, aggregation follows the employee's W-4 and often withholds more on a large commission.
  • A draw against commission is usually regular pay; the commission above the draw is supplemental.

States

State treatment varies. Some states set their own flat supplemental rate, some require aggregation, and some leave it open. See supplemental withholding by state.

Questions

How are commissions taxed?

As supplemental wages. Federal withholding can use the flat 22% rate if the commission is separately identified, or aggregate it with regular pay. FICA applies in full.

Why is my commission check withheld more than expected?

If it is aggregated with regular pay, the combined check annualises to a higher bracket, which withholds more. The final tax is settled on the return.
