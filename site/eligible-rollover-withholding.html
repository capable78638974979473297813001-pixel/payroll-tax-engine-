Eligible rollover and nonperiodic payment withholding

Lump sums and rollovers are withheld at a rate, not by a worksheet. The rate depends on the type of payment and what the payee chose.

Payments that are not periodic installments are withheld at a flat percentage of the taxable amount. The payee sets the rate on Form W-4R; with no form, the default applies.

The rates

PaymentDefaultThe payee can choose
Nonperiodic payment, including an IRA distribution payable on demand10%Any whole percent from 0% to 100% (not less than 10% if delivered outside the United States and its territories)
Eligible rollover distribution (qualified plan, 403(b), governmental 457(b))20%A higher whole percent. Never lower than 20%, including 0%

With no Form W-4R, or no valid taxpayer ID, the payer withholds the default and cannot honor a request for less.

Only the taxable part

Withholding applies to the taxable amount of the payment. If part is a return of after-tax contributions, send pension.taxableAmount and the rate applies to that part only.

What counts as an eligible rollover distribution

Form W-4R lists payments that are not eligible rollover distributions for these rules: hardship distributions, required minimum distributions, distributions from a pension-linked emergency savings account, and several qualified disaster, birth or adoption, long-term care, domestic abuse and emergency-expense distributions. Those are nonperiodic payments, so send them as nonperiodic; whether a payment is one or the other is a fact about the plan that you supply.

Questions

How much is withheld from an eligible rollover distribution?

The payer must withhold 20% of the taxable amount unless the payment is rolled over directly. The payee can ask for a higher rate on Form W-4R but not a lower one, including zero.

What is the default withholding on a 401(k) or IRA lump sum?

An IRA distribution payable on demand or any other nonperiodic payment defaults to 10%, and the payee can choose 0% to 100%. A lump sum from a 401(k) that could be rolled over is an eligible rollover distribution and defaults to 20%.

Can a payee choose no withholding?

On a nonperiodic payment, yes, by entering 0% on Form W-4R, unless the payment is delivered outside the United States and its territories. On an eligible rollover distribution, no.
