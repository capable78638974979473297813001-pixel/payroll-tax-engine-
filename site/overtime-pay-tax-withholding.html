Withholding and wages

Overtime pay tax withholding

Overtime is not taxed at a special rate. It just makes the check bigger.

There is no separate overtime tax rate. Overtime pay is wages: federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare apply as they do to regular pay. A large overtime check can feel heavily taxed because the percentage method annualises the check, treating a big one-off period as a regular high income.

Regular and overtime, side by side

GrossFederal income taxNet pay
Regular pay only$3,000.00$320.38$2,450.12
Plus a $1,500.00 overtime line$4,500.00$650.38$3,505.37

Adding $1,500.00 of overtime raises federal withholding by $330.00, which is 22.0% of the overtime, because the extra pay sits in a higher bracket in the annualised calculation. It is the same rate the employee would pay on any $4,500.00 check.

Why it looks like more

  • The percentage method assumes every check is like this one, so a bigger check annualises to a bigger yearly income.
  • The employee's actual tax for the year is settled on their return, where overtime is taxed like other income.
  • A pay stub that shows overtime separately makes the withheld percentage look higher than the stated rate.

A flag, for one state

The earning has an overtime flag. Almost every jurisdiction ignores it. One state rule that excluded overtime from taxable wages (Alabama, for a limited period in 2024 and 2025) reads it. Outside such a rule, overtime is just wages of its category.

Categorise it

The IRS lists overtime pay among supplemental wages when it is paid separately from regular wages, and treats it as part of regular wages when paid in the same check. Send overtime as regular to withhold with the aggregate method, or as supplemental to use the flat supplemental rate, per your policy. See flat rate vs aggregate.

Questions

Is overtime taxed at a higher rate?

No. Overtime is ordinary wages. It looks higher because a bigger check annualises to a higher bracket in the withholding calculation, but the employee's actual tax is settled on their return.

Are Social Security and Medicare taken from overtime?

Yes. They apply to all wages up to the Social Security wage base.
