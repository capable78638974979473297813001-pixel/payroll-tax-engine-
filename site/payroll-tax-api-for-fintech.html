Payroll tax API for fintech

Turn a gross pay figure into a take-home estimate you can lend against.

Fintech products meet payroll data in many forms: a gross salary on an application, a pay frequency, a state. The decision they need is about net pay, because that is what a person can actually spend. A rule of thumb like "gross times 0.75" ignores the state, the filing status and the pay frequency, and it is wrong by amounts that matter in an affordability check.

Uses

  • Earned wage access: how much of an accrued wage is safely available after taxes.
  • Affordability checks: net monthly pay for a loan or buy-now-pay-later decision.
  • Benefits and savings products: how a pre-tax contribution changes take-home.
  • Gross-up: the gross pay that leaves a target net, using the net-to-gross endpoint.

Why a flat percentage fails

Run the same $3,000.00 biweekly pay through two states. Texas returns $2,450.12 and California returns $2,277.06. As a share of gross that is 81.7% and 75.9%. A single multiplier cannot be right for both.

Inputs you usually have

  • Gross pay and pay frequency from the pay stub or the employer.
  • State, and ideally a work address for local tax.
  • Filing status. If you do not have a W-4, state your assumption (for example, single with no adjustments) and say so to the user.

Be clear it is an estimate

Withholding follows the employee's W-4 and the employer's choices, so a calculated net pay is an estimate of an actual paycheck. Show the assumptions you made, and for a credit decision prefer verified pay data where you can get it. The API calculates tax and does not verify income.

Questions

Can a payroll tax API support affordability checks?

It can turn a gross pay, pay frequency and location into a net pay estimate that follows federal, state and local rules, which is more accurate than a flat percentage. It estimates withholding; it does not verify income.

What do I do without the employee's W-4?

Assume a stated default such as single filing status with no adjustments, and tell the user that is the assumption.
