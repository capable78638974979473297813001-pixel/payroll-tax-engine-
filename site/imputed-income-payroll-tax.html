Withholding and wages

Imputed income and payroll tax

Taxable pay the employee never receives as cash.

Imputed income is the taxable value of a benefit that is not paid in cash. The usual example is employer-paid group-term life insurance above $50,000 of coverage. It is added to taxable wages, so tax is withheld on it, but no money is paid. The employee's net pay goes down.

A paycheck with imputed income

A $120.00 imputed amount on the $3,000.00 check:

WithoutWith imputed income
Cash gross pay$3,000.00$3,000.00
Federal taxable wages$3,000.00$3,120.00
Federal income tax$320.38$346.78
Social Security$186.00$193.44
Net pay$2,450.12$2,414.54

Taxable wages rise to $3,120.00, tax rises, and net pay falls by $35.58 even though no cash was added.

Where it comes from

  • Group-term life insurance above $50,000 of coverage.
  • Personal use of a company vehicle, in the cases where it is taxable.
  • Some other fringe benefits that are taxable and not paid in cash.

The value comes from the IRS tables or the benefit rules, which your benefits system supplies. The engine applies the tax to the amount you send.

A limit

Because imputed income adds tax without cash, it cannot raise take-home pay. The gross-up endpoint refuses to solve for an imputed earning with the code cannot_gross_up.

Questions

What is imputed income?

The taxable value of a benefit that is not paid in cash, such as group-term life insurance over $50,000. It is added to taxable wages, so tax is withheld, while net pay falls.

How do I send it to the API?

As an earning with category imputed. It raises the taxable wages but is not paid in cash.
