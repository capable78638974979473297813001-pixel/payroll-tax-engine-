California SDI withholding

The employee-paid disability and paid family leave contribution.

California State Disability Insurance (SDI) is an employee-paid payroll contribution that funds both disability insurance and Paid Family Leave. California has no separate paid-leave payroll tax: the same contribution covers both. Since 2024 it applies to all wages, with no yearly cap. Employers do not pay it, which surprises payroll teams used to employer-paid unemployment tax.

On a $3,000.00 biweekly check in California

The engine returns $39.00 on taxable wages of $3,000.00, paid by the employee. In its own words: $3000.00 @ 1.30%, no wage cap

TaxTaxable wagesAmount
California Disability Benefits (Employee)$3,000.00$39.00

Who pays

The employee. It reduces net pay and has no employer match, which is why it is in the employee total and not the employer total in the response.

Points to remember

  • It is a separate line from California income tax (CA_SIT).
  • There is no wage cap, so the line never stops during the year and no year-to-date field is needed.
  • Which deductions reduce the SDI base is set by the state's own rule file, and the engine applies it by category.
  • The rate is set each year, so the check date picks the right one.
  • The employer still owes California unemployment tax, which appears as an employer line.

In the API

The line comes back from POST /v1/paycheck with workState.code set to CA, as CA_DBL_EE with payer: "employee". No extra field is needed to turn it on. See California payroll tax for the rest of the state.

Questions

Who pays California SDI?

The employee. It is withheld from wages and the employer does not match it.

Does California SDI have a wage cap?

No. Since January 1, 2024 all wages are subject to SDI contributions.

Is SDI the same as California income tax?

No. It is a separate contribution with its own rate, and it appears as its own line next to California income tax.
