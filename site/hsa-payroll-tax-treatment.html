HSA contributions and payroll tax

Through a cafeteria plan, an HSA is pre-tax for everything.

A health savings account contribution made through payroll under a cafeteria plan (Section 125) is excluded from federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare wages. That is better than a 401(k) deferral, which escapes only income tax. The engine encodes this by category.

The employer saves too

Employer Social Security and Medicare are also computed on the lower wage: $328.50 with no deduction against $295.65 with the HSA.

A state exception to watch

A few states do not follow the federal exclusion for HSA contributions. The state rule files record the treatment tax by tax, and the engine applies it, so a contribution can be pre-tax federally and taxable for that state. See deduction treatment by tax.

Questions

Is an HSA contribution through payroll subject to FICA?

Not when made through a Section 125 cafeteria plan. It is excluded from Social Security and Medicare wages as well as federal income tax wages.

Does every state exclude HSA contributions?

No. A few states tax them. The engine applies each state's own rule by tax.
