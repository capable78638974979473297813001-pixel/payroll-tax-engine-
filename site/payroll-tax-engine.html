Payroll tax engine

The component that knows the tax rules, and the questions to ask before you pick one.

A payroll tax engine is a service or library that calculates the taxes on a paycheck. Payroll platforms, HR systems and staffing software use one so they do not have to maintain the federal, state and local rules themselves.

Who needs one

  • A team building payroll or HR software that needs gross-to-net without owning the rules.
  • A business with its own payroll process that needs to check or estimate withholding by location.
  • A finance team modelling the cost of a hire in another state, including the employer's side.

What an engine covers

Federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state and local income tax, employer unemployment tax, and state paid-leave contributions. Omnia.tax adds garnishment limits and a minimum-wage check on the same call.

What it does not

Filing returns, depositing tax, paying employees and advising. Those are separate services. If you need them in one vendor, see the comparison of payroll tax engines.

How to choose

  • Check the local layer first, and how the right jurisdiction is found from an address. Ours: 4,141 local jurisdictions in 15 states, listed by name on coverage, found by address geocoding.
  • Ask for a worked example with your own edge cases: a bonus, a multi-state employee, a pre-tax deduction.
  • Ask what happens when a state changes a rate mid-year.
  • Ask for the price at your volume. Ours is published: $0.08 per paycheck calculation.

Questions

What is a payroll tax engine?

A service that calculates the taxes on a paycheck: federal, state and local withholding, Social Security, Medicare and employer taxes. It is the rules component inside payroll software.

How do I choose a payroll tax engine?

Compare local-tax coverage, how it handles effective dates and mid-year changes, whether each line is explained, whether you need filing as well as calculation, and the price per calculation at your volume.
