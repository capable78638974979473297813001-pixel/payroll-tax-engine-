Withholding and wages

Pay frequency and withholding

Pay frequency changes the table, and the table changes the tax.

Federal withholding annualises each paycheck: multiply by the number of pay periods in the year, apply the tax brackets, then divide back. So $3,000.00 paid weekly is a $156,000.00 yearly income; paid monthly it is only $36,000.00. The same check withholds very different amounts.

One check, four frequencies

Pay frequencyPeriodsAnnualised wagesFederal income taxNet pay
Weekly52$156,000.00$503.35$2,267.15
Biweekly26$78,000.00$320.38$2,450.12
Semimonthly24$72,000.00$292.08$2,478.42
Monthly12$36,000.00$178.33$2,592.17

Every row is a $3,000.00 check for a single filer in Texas. Social Security and Medicare are identical in all four, at $186.00 and $43.50, because they are flat percentages.

What this means

  • A comparison of "take-home per month" across employers must use the same schedule, or the annualised tax will not be comparable.
  • An employee who moves from monthly to weekly pay with the same annual salary has a similar yearly tax, because each check is smaller.
  • The annual tax is settled on the return, so withholding differences by schedule even out over the year when pay is steady.

In the request

Set payFrequency to weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, monthly, quarterly, semiannual, annual or daily. State tables use the same frequency.

Questions

Why does pay frequency change federal withholding?

The percentage method annualises each check by the number of pay periods, applies the brackets, and divides back. A larger check on a weekly schedule annualises to a higher income than the same check paid monthly.

Does pay frequency change Social Security or Medicare?

No. They are flat percentages of wages, so the amount is the same on any schedule.
