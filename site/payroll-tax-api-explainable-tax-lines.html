Explainable payroll tax lines

A figure you cannot explain is a figure you cannot defend.

A payroll tax number with no reasoning attached is hard to trust and impossible to dispute. When an employee asks why their withholding changed, or an auditor asks how a figure was reached, "the system said so" is not an answer. Each Omnia.tax tax line carries a detail sentence that states the method and the intermediate numbers.

What the detail says

For the Columbus, Ohio paycheck of $3,000.00, the federal income tax line reads:

annualised $78000.00 → adjusted $69400.00; bracket 22% over $57900.00; tentative/yr $8330.00 ÷ 26

That is the whole calculation: the annualised wages, the adjusted wages after the filing-status adjustment, the bracket, the tentative annual tax and the number of pay periods it was divided by. Check it against IRS Publication 15-T and it ties.

Other lines explain themselves too

  • Social Security ($186.00): $3000.00 @ 6.2%
  • Medicare ($43.50): $3000.00 @ 1.45%, no wage cap
  • Ohio Income Tax ($75.77): $3000.00 less $0.00 exemptions (0 × $25) = $3000.00 net @ 2.99% over $1001.92, base $16.03
  • Ohio Municipal Income Tax ($75.00): $75.00 to Columbus @ 2.50% (residence and work municipality are the same)

Who uses it

  • Payroll specialists reconciling the engine against a legacy system line by line.
  • Support teams answering "why is my check lower this week".
  • Auditors who want the method, not just the result.
  • Developers who need to show a user the reason, not only the number.

When the source is weaker

Not every rate has the same quality of source. Where a rate rests on something weaker than the levying body's own form, the response adds a notices entry naming the tax and the tier. See data quality notices.

Questions

What is an explainable payroll tax line?

A tax line that states how its amount was reached: the method, the wages it applied to and the intermediate figures, so a person can audit it.

Can I show the detail text to employees?

Yes. It is plain language written for payroll specialists, and you can show it on a pay stub explanation or in a support tool.
