Withholding and wages

W-4 dependents credit (Step 3)

An annual credit, spread evenly across the year's paychecks.

Step 3 of the Form W-4 lets an employee claim credits for qualifying children and other dependents. The employer does not apply the credit to wages. They subtract it from the tentative tax, spread over the pay periods in the year. So a $4,000.00 annual credit on a biweekly schedule lowers each check's federal withholding by $4,000.00 divided by 26.

Points that catch people

  • The amount is the Step 3 total for the year, not per check.
  • The credit cannot reduce federal withholding below zero.
  • It does not change Social Security or Medicare.
  • The pay frequency matters: the same annual credit gives a larger per-check reduction on a monthly schedule than a weekly one.

Questions

How does the W-4 Step 3 credit change my paycheck?

The annual credit is divided by the number of pay periods and subtracted from each check's federal income tax withholding. A $4,000 credit on a biweekly schedule lowers each check by about $153.85.

Does the credit affect Social Security or Medicare tax?

No. It only reduces federal income tax withholding.
