FMLA eligibility

    
The 3-part test (12 months employed, 1,250 hours of service in the past 12 months, 50+ employees within 75 miles of the worksite) — all three must hold. This project doesn't track employment history or worksite geography, so each figure is entered directly.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Workers' comp premium estimate

    
Standard NCCI formula: (subject payroll ÷ 100) × classification rate × experience mod. The overtime PREMIUM portion (the extra half of time-and-a-half) is excluded from subject payroll everywhere except Pennsylvania and Delaware. Class codes, rates, and the experience mod are all figures only an insurer or rating bureau assigns — entered directly, not looked up.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California paid sick leave

    
California only — accrual is 1 hour per 30 hours worked, capped at 80 hours; use is capped at 40 hours/year even if the balance is higher; and leave can't be used until the 90th day of employment. This project doesn't track cumulative hours worked or a running CA sick-leave balance, so both are entered directly.

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
401(k) elective deferral limit

    
2026 IRS limits: $24,500 base, +$8,000 catch-up at 50+, or +$11,250 enhanced catch-up for ages 60-63 specifically (SECURE 2.0). A participant 50+ whose prior-year FICA wages from this employer exceeded $150,000 must make all catch-up contributions as Roth. This project doesn't track employee age or a running elective-deferral YTD total, so all three are entered directly.

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
HSA / FSA contribution limits

    
2026 IRS limits: HSA $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family, +$1,000 catch-up at 55+ with no upper age cutoff; health FSA $3,400 (up to $680 may carry over, plan-dependent); dependent-care FSA $7,500 ($3,750 married filing separately, per OBBBA). This project doesn't verify HDHP eligibility itself or track running YTD contributions, so all figures are entered directly.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Federal WARN Act

    
Covers employers with 100+ employees (excluding part-time). A plant closing needs 50+ affected employees at one site; a mass layoff needs 500+, or 50+ AND at least one-third of the site's workforce. 60 days' advance written notice is required. This project doesn't track a "layoff event" spanning multiple employees, so every count is entered directly. Federal only — many states run their own stricter mini-WARN laws, not modeled here.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California meal & rest breaks

    
A first meal period is required past 5 hours worked (waivable at or under 6); a second past 10 hours (waivable at or under 12, only if the first wasn't waived). Rest breaks follow "10 minutes per 4 hours or major fraction thereof." Each of the meal/rest premiums is capped at one hour of pay per workday regardless of how many breaks were missed that day — enter whether ANY violation of that type happened, not a count. This project doesn't detect break timing from punch data, so hours worked and violation flags are entered directly.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
New York wage notice (WTPA)

    
Checks a Notice and Acknowledgement of Pay Rate and Payday for every field NY Labor Law § 195(1) requires, whether a rate change needs a brand-new notice, and the DOL's own per-day penalty exposure ($50/day for a notice violation, $250/day for a wage-statement violation, each capped at $5,000/worker). This project doesn't generate or translate the notice document itself.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
FLSA overtime exemption salary level

    
Checks ONLY the salary/compensation floor DOL currently publishes: $684/week standard (executive, administrative, professional), $684/week OR $27.63/hour for computer employees, $684/week PLUS $107,432/year total comp for highly compensated, and no floor at all for outside sales. This is necessary but never sufficient for exempt status — the duties test and salary-basis test are fact-specific judgments this project doesn't evaluate.

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California bereavement leave

    
Covers employers with 5+ employees, for an employee with 30+ days of tenure, up to 5 days per death of a spouse/child/parent/sibling/grandparent/grandchild/domestic partner/parent-in-law, completed within 3 months of the death. Only paid to whatever extent an existing policy already pays for it — the remainder is unpaid. This project has no leave-request workflow for this leave type, so dates and policy figures are entered directly.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Federal PUMP Act

    
Break time to express milk is required for 1 year after the child's birth. Employers under 50 employees (counted across all worksites) may qualify for an undue-hardship exemption — this only checks the headcount half of that test, not the fact-specific hardship showing. Pay is required unless the employee is completely relieved of duty AND the break doesn't coincide with an already-paid break. The space must be shielded from view, free from intrusion, and never a bathroom.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Backup withholding

    
24% federal withholding when a payee has no valid TIN on file. The IRS's own awaiting-TIN 60-day grace period applies ONLY to interest/dividend payments — nonemployee compensation (1099-NEC contractor payments) is withheld immediately with no grace period at all, even while a TIN application is pending. This project doesn't model the B-notice procedural workflow (CP2100/CP2100A mismatch notices).

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Pregnant Workers Fairness Act

    
Covers employers with 15+ employees, in effect since June 27, 2023. Four specific accommodations (water access, additional restroom breaks, sitting/standing as needed, additional eating/drinking breaks) are "predictable assessments" the EEOC says are reasonable in virtually every case — no medical documentation may be required for those. Every other accommodation still goes through PWFA's general reasonable-accommodation-unless-undue-hardship standard, which this project doesn't evaluate (a fact-specific judgment, same as the ADA's own).

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
CalSavers

    
Effective January 1, 2026, every California employer with 1+ employees and no qualified retirement plan of its own must register with CalSavers or certify an exemption. Default auto-enrollment starts at 5%, escalating 1 point/year up to 8%. FTB penalties run $250/eligible employee, plus another $500/employee (total $750) once noncompliance continues. See the Illinois Secure Choice and OregonSaves panels below for those states' own genuinely different coverage tests and penalty structures.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
USERRA (military leave reemployment)

    
Applies to every employer regardless of size — no small-employer exemption at all. The application deadline depends purely on how long the service lasted: under 31 days means reporting at the start of the next scheduled work period; 31-180 days means applying within 14 days of release; over 180 days means applying within 90 days. Health coverage may be continued for up to 24 months at up to 102% of premium (service over 30 days only — shorter service continues coverage automatically).

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California itemized wage statement

    
Checks a pay stub against every item Labor Code § 226(a) requires — this project's own paystub renderer doesn't currently include total hours worked, hourly rates and hours at each rate, the last 4 of the employee's SSN, or the employer's address, so those are entered directly here. Penalty exposure (a "knowing and intentional" failure only, not evaluated here) is $50 for the first affected pay period, $100 for each after that, capped at $4,000/employee.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Illinois Secure Choice

    
Covers employers with 5+ employees AND 2+ years in business AND no qualified retirement plan — all three conditions, unlike CalSavers' own single headcount test. Default auto-enrollment is 5% (no confirmed automatic escalation, unlike CalSavers). Penalties run $250/employee for the first noncompliant calendar year and $500/employee for each subsequent one (need not be consecutive) — a different structure from CalSavers' own. Curing within 120 days of an IDOR notice avoids the assessment.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
OregonSaves

    
The simplest coverage test of the three auto-IRA programs modeled here: NO employee-count threshold beyond 1+ employees, and NO years-in-business test at all. Default auto-enrollment is 5%, escalating 1 point/year up to a 10% ceiling (five years to reach it) — the same escalation shape as CalSavers, but a different cap (10% vs. 8%). Penalties run $100/affected employee, but CAPPED at $5,000 per calendar year per employer — unlike CalSavers' or Illinois Secure Choice's own uncapped per-employee penalties.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

  


  

    
FLSA tip credit and tip pooling

    
Wires src/minimum-wage.ts directly rather than re-deriving any state's own tip-credit rules here — that engine already knows which states (CA, MN, MT, NV, OR, WA, AK, Guam, Flagstaff AZ) allow NO tip credit at all. Federal max tip credit is $5.12/hour ($7.25 - $2.13). If actual cash wages plus tips received fall short of the full minimum wage for the hours worked, the employer owes the exact gap. Tip pool eligibility: a manager or supervisor is NEVER eligible, regardless of tip-credit status; where the employer takes a tip credit, only customarily-tipped employees may be pooled (back-of-house excluded); with no tip credit taken, every non-management employee is eligible.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California pay data reporting

    
Every constant here is copied verbatim from CRD's own Reporting Year 2025 handbook, since earlier research surfaced conflicting third-party figures for the top pay band boundary ($208,000 vs. $239,200) that only the primary source resolved. Two independent 100-employee tests — a payroll employee report and a labor contractor employee report — never combined for threshold purposes. 12 pay bands based on W-2 Box 5 earnings. Filing deadline is the second Wednesday of May, the year after the reporting year. Penalties run up to $100/employee for a first failure to file, up to $200/employee for a subsequent one.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
State mini-WARN acts

    
Three states only, deliberately. New York keeps federal WARN's own two-branch shape (flat plant-closing threshold, separate percentage-based mass-layoff formula) at half federal's numbers, with a 90-day notice period. New Jersey and California each use a SINGLE flat threshold instead, no percentage test at all — New Jersey counts its 100-employee employer threshold NATIONWIDE (any status), and uniquely requires mandatory severance (1 week/year of service) regardless of notice adequacy, plus a 4-week penalty if notice was inadequate. California adds an independent 100-mile relocation trigger. Every other state's own mini-WARN law remains a disclosed gap.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Oregon Fair Work Week Act

    
Oregon is the only STATEWIDE predictive-scheduling law (Chicago, NYC, Philadelphia, SF, and Seattle each run their own separate city ordinances, not modeled here). Covers employers with 500+ employees WORLDWIDE, for non-exempt hourly employees only. Two genuinely different pay formulas: an ADDITIVE change (adding time, or a new shift) owes a flat ONE HOUR of pay regardless of how much was added; a SUBTRACTIVE change (cutting hours) owes HALF the regular rate for EACH scheduled hour not worked — this one scales with the size of the cut. A change of 30 minutes or less owes nothing. Shifts must have at least 10 hours of rest between them; hours worked during that rest period are paid at 1.5x even with consent.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
SECURE 2.0 auto-enrollment mandate

    
Two dates matter, not one: a plan must have been established on or after 2022-12-29 (the Act's own enactment date — everything before it is permanently grandfathered), AND the mandate itself only bites for plan years beginning on or after 2025-01-01. Six independent exemptions: pre-enactment plans, fewer than 10 employees, fewer than 3 years in business, governmental plans, church plans, and SIMPLE 401(k) plans. The initial default rate must be 3%-10%, escalating at least 1 point/year to a sponsor-chosen ceiling between 10%-15%. Auto-enrolled participants may withdraw their default contributions penalty-free within 90 days of the first one.

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
New York Paid Family Leave

    
Full-time (20+ hrs/week) eligible after 26 consecutive weeks; part-time eligible after 175 days worked (need not be consecutive). The weekly benefit is 67% of the employee's own average weekly wage, capped at 67% of the 2026 statewide average ($1,833.63/week), for $1,228.53 max/week. This project's core tax engine already computes the PFL payroll withholding correctly (data/states/NY-2026.json) — this panel is the benefit side only, which isn't modeled anywhere else.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

  


  

    
New Jersey Family Leave Insurance

    
Eligible via 20+ base weeks each earning $310+, OR $15,500+ in total base-year earnings. The benefit is 85% of average weekly wage, capped at $1,119/week for 2026. Up to 12 weeks continuous, or up to 56 days (8 weeks) intermittent, within a 12-month period — separate caps, not additive. As with NY PFL, this project's core tax engine already computes the FLI payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Massachusetts PFML

    
A genuinely different, TIERED benefit formula: 80% of average weekly wage up to 50% of the 2026 state average weekly wage ($961.24), plus 50% of any amount above that, capped at $1,230.39/week. Eligible only if base-period earnings are both at least $6,300 AND strictly more than 30x the computed weekly benefit. This project's core tax engine already computes the PFML payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California Family Rights Act (CFRA)

    
Covers employers with just 5+ employees statewide — much lower than FMLA's own 50-employee/75-mile worksite test, which CFRA eliminated in 2021. Tenure must be MORE than 12 months (exactly 12 does not qualify, unlike FMLA's inclusive test), plus 1,250 hours of service. 12 weeks of leave, scaled by the employee's own schedule. An employer may limit "designated person" leave to 1 person per 12-month period.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Washington PFML

    
Eligible with 820+ hours worked in a rolling 12-month qualifying period. A third distinct benefit formula in this project: 90% of average weekly wage up to half the 2026 state average weekly wage ($915), plus 50% above that, capped at $1,647/week — but with its own $100/week floor, except an employee earning under $100/week gets their full wage instead of the floor. This project's core tax engine already computes the PFML payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Colorado FAMLI

    
A fourth two-tier PFML benefit formula: 90% of average weekly wage up to 50% of the 2026-2027 state average weekly wage ($804.46), plus 50% above that, capped at $1,448.02/week (90% of the full state average weekly wage). Eligibility is a single, simple test — at least $2,500 in base-period earnings from any combination of employers, with no minimum tenure or hours-worked requirement. Standard leave is 12 weeks, extending to 16 for pregnancy or childbirth complications. This project's core tax engine already computes the FAMLI payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Paid Leave Oregon

    
A genuinely different shape from every other state PFL module here: 100% wage replacement (not a fraction) up to 65% of the 2026-2027 state average weekly wage ($916.58), then 50% of any amount above that. Both the floor ($70.51) and the cap ($1,692.16) are defined as percentages of the state average weekly wage (5% and 120%) rather than separately-published flat figures. Eligible with at least $1,000 in base-year Oregon wages — no other test. Standard leave is 12 weeks, extending to 14 for a pregnancy/childbirth-related medical condition (available only to the parent who gave birth). This project's core tax engine already computes the payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Connecticut Paid Leave

    
The only sibling formula pegged to the state MINIMUM WAGE rather than a state average weekly wage: 95% replacement up to 40x the 2026 CT minimum wage ($677.60), then 60% of the excess, capped at 60x the minimum wage ($1,016.40) — both figures rise automatically whenever the minimum wage does. Eligibility is TWO independent tests: at least $2,325 in the single highest-earning quarter of the base period, AND currently employed or separated within the past 12 weeks. This project's core tax engine already computes the payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Maine PFML

    
The only sibling module that rounds DOWN to the whole dollar at every step (matching Maine's own published worksheet), not to the nearest cent: 90% replacement up to 50% of the 2026-2027 state average weekly wage ($624, floored), then 66% of the excess, capped at the full state average weekly wage ($1,249.12) — not a percentage of it. Eligible with base-period earnings of at least 6x the state average weekly wage ($7,494.72) — no other test. All covered reasons draw on the same combined 12-week annual allowance. This project's core tax engine already computes the payroll withholding correctly; this panel is the benefit side only.

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
State non-compete bans and thresholds

    
Six jurisdictions only, deliberately: California, North Dakota, and Oklahoma void every employee non-compete outright; Minnesota's own ban is NOT retroactive (void only from 2023-07-01 forward). Colorado, Illinois, Washington, and Oregon instead use FOUR genuinely different threshold mechanisms — Colorado derives its non-solicit threshold as a fixed 60% of its non-compete threshold; Illinois uses two fixed dollar figures on a step schedule (not CPI-adjusted); Washington publishes separate figures for employees vs. independent contractors, and its own threshold regime sunsets to a near-total ban on 2027-06-30; Oregon requires a salary threshold AND a 12-month max term AND 14 days' advance written notice, all three at once. Every other state's own rule is a disclosed gap.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
COBRA general notice

    
Distinct from the election notice above: due within 90 days of first becoming covered under the plan, or sooner if an election notice is already required by then. Penalty exposure: up to $110/day per affected beneficiary (ERISA §502(c)(1), court-assessed, not inflation-adjusted), plus a separate IRS excise tax of $100/day (or $200/day if more than one family member is affected — not the single rate doubled).

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California Fair Chance Act (ban the box)

    
Covers employers with 5+ employees. Criminal history may only be inquired about once a conditional offer has been made. If rescinding an offer over conviction history, an applicant gets at least 5 business days to respond, extended by 5 more if they dispute the report. A mailed notice without delivery confirmation is deemed received 5/10/20 calendar days later depending on the address. This project doesn't evaluate the individualized-assessment factors themselves (nature of offense, time elapsed, nature of job) — a fact-specific judgment.

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
California salary history ban / pay scale transparency

    
The salary history inquiry ban and the on-request pay-scale disclosures (to applicants and current employees) apply to EVERY California employer, regardless of size. Only the job-POSTING pay-scale requirement has a 15-employee floor. A first job-posting violation carries no penalty if all postings are demonstrably updated; otherwise the Labor Commissioner's civil penalty runs $100-$10,000.

    

      
      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

  


  

    
New York pay transparency

    
Labor Law § 194-b: employers with 4+ employees must disclose compensation ranges and job descriptions in postings for jobs, promotions, and transfers performed at least in part in New York. Flat civil penalty tiers — $1,000 first violation, $2,000 second, $3,000 for the third and every one after (never escalating further). A genuinely different penalty shape from California's own $100-$10,000 discretionary range.

    

      
      
      
    

    

  


  

    
Colorado Equal Pay for Equal Work Act (Part 2)

    
C.R.S. §§ 8-5-201 to 8-5-203: NO employer-size threshold at all for job-posting compensation disclosure or promotional-opportunity notice — any employer with a Colorado job opening is covered. The one carve-out is temporal and remote-workforce-based, not size-based: through July 1, 2029, an out-of-state employer with fewer than 15 fully-remote Colorado employees owes notice only for remote openings. Fines run $500-$10,000 per violation; a written complaint may be filed within one year of learning of a violation.

California waiting time penalty

    
Cal. Labor Code § 203: a full day's wages (hourly rate × normal daily hours) for every CALENDAR day a final paycheck is late — weekends and holidays count — capped at 30 days. A good-faith dispute over the wages owed can be a full defense, which this project doesn't evaluate (a fact-specific judgment about the employer's own state of mind).
